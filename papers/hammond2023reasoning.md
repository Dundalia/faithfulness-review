**TL;DR** Unifies causal models and game theory, defining counterfactuals for adaptive agents while preserving standard equilibria and enabling more compact game analysis.
**Problem** Standard causal models omit strategic adaptation; standard games leave causal assumptions implicit.
**Method** Mechanised causal games, rationality relations, intervention/counterfactual semantics; graphical relevance proofs, equilibrium correspondences, PyCID examples.
**Findings** Distinguishes policy adaptation from fixed-policy effects; supports multiple rational outcomes; subgames can accelerate equilibrium computation.
**Limits** Common prior, known mechanisms, Markovian counterfactual models; cross-world randomization and policy invariance require assumptions.
