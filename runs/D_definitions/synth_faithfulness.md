### 1. Accurate representation of the reasoning process

This is the umbrella answer: an explanation or CoT is faithful when it accurately represents, reflects or describes the process by which the model reached its output. ### 2. Disclosure of the factors that drove the answer

Here a trace is faithful when it states the factors that moved the answer, and the standard test inserts a cue (a suggested answer, a metadata hint, a planted bias), keeps the cases where the answer moves to the cued option, and asks whether the CoT mentions the cue and, in stricter versions, whether it presents the cue as a reason. Of the 433 rows, 168 are operational (judge rubrics, keyword detectors, cue taxonomies, noise corrections) and 72 are failure modes (silent re-derivation of the cued answer, laundering of the cue's argument, unverbalized reward hacks), and variants move the factor from planted cues to concepts ([Matton et al. 2025](./#p=matton2025walk); [Arcuschin et al. 2026](./#p=arcuschin2026biases)), to factor-level reports ([Zhang et al. 2026](./#p=zhang2026audit)) and to pairs of logically opposite questions ([Arcuschin et al. 2025](./#p=arcuschin2025chain)).

### 3. Causal dependence of the answer on the stated reasoning

Here a trace is faithful to the extent that the answer depends on it: truncating it, inserting a mistake, paraphrasing it, replacing it with filler, unlearning a step from the weights or patching in another chain should change the answer. Post-hoc reasoning, produced after the answer is fixed, is the central failure, and 63 of the 402 rows are failure modes (post-hoc rationalization, reasoning theater, decorative and performative CoT). [Lanham et al. 2023](./#p=lanham2023measuring)'s tests are the template; later rows recast them in mediation terms, move the intervention into the weights ([Tutek et al. 2025](./#p=tutek2025measuring)), locate the answer before the CoT with probes ([Kudo et al. 2024](./#p=kudo2024llms); [Cox et al. 2026](./#p=cox2026posthoc)), or set validity conditions that an intervention must pass before its effect is read as faithfulness ([Anonymous 2026 (CRISP)](./#p=anon2026crisp)).

> A reasoning trace is faithful if the model’s answer causally depends on the intermediate reasoning steps.
>
> ([Swaroop et al. 2025](./#p=swaroop2025lift), §2)

### 4. Agreement between the stated reasoning and the answer

Here faithfulness is read off the output (the answer follows from, is entailed by or agrees with what the reasoning says), and rows build systems in which a solver or a selection-inference pipeline derives the answer from the stated chain (faithful by construction), measure reasoning–answer consistency with judges or NLI, or also require the reasoning to be valid, so that a correct answer reached through a flawed chain counts as unfaithful. 121 of the 147 rows are proposed, and the 27 first-pass rows on whether an agent's action follows its stated reasoning or plan now form cluster 5.

> To our knowledge, this is the first system to generate multistep chains that are both faithful (the answer follows from the reasoning) and truthful (the chain reflects the system’s own internal beliefs).
>
> ([Tafjord et al. 2022](./#p=tafjord2022entailer), Abstract)

### 6. Predictive value: the explanation lets an observer predict the model

### 8. Input importance: the explanation marks the inputs the prediction depends on

**Jacovi & Goldberg's sentence and its paraphrases.** 65 rows from 54 papers cite [Jacovi & Goldberg 2020](./#p=jacovi2020towards) outside cluster 11, where 14 more rows cite them for a companion term. Jacovi & Goldberg word the definition two ways, "accurately represents the reasoning process behind the model’s prediction" and "how accurately it reflects the true reasoning process of the model"; 21 of the 65 rows keep "reasoning process" and 8 keep "accurately represent", and the substitutions in the others point toward different clusters:

- toward internal computation: "the reasoning accurately describe the models internal computation" ([Lewis-Lim et al. 2026](./#p=lewislim2026efficient)), "the degree to which its reasoning traces reflect its internal decision-making" ([Cheah et al. 2026](./#p=cheah2026training));
- toward behavior: "explanations should reflect how the model actually behaves, not just sound plausible" ([Malladi & Srivastava 2026](./#p=malladi2026directly)), "whether explanations support actual model behavior" ([Hase et al. 2020](./#p=hase2020leakageadjusted));
- toward causal role: "the degree to which a model’s stated reasoning genuinely causes its final prediction, rather than merely accompanying it" ([Somov et al. 2026](./#p=somov2026breaking));
- toward factors: "An explanation is faithful if it reflects the factors behind a model’s prediction" ([Anonymous 2026 (one draw)](./#p=anon2026one)).

[Chen et al. 2025](./#p=chen2025reasoning) adapt the sentence in two ways at once (the attribution check marks it adapted): they relax it to "the key decision factors and reasoning chains" and add that these should "enable us to predict how it would process similar inputs during deployment", and their operational test uses only the first part.

**Plausibility drifts from persuasiveness to correctness.** For Jacovi & Goldberg plausibility is "how convincing the interpretation is to humans", and [Lyu et al. 2023](./#p=lyu2023faithful) keep that sense ("a.k.a. persuasiveness or understandability"). [Chen et al. 2023](./#p=pmlr-v235-chen24bl) write instead that "Plausibility evaluates humans’ preference of an explanation based on its factual correctness and logical coherence", citing Herman 2017, Lage et al. 2019 and Jacovi & Goldberg; the same sentence recurs in [Chen et al. 2024](./#p=chen2025consistent), [Chen et al. 2025](./#p=chen2025reasoning) have "the factual correctness of a CoT and how convincingly it justifies the model’s response", and [Cahlik et al. 2025](./#p=cahlik2025reasoninggrounded) reduce plausibility to whether "the explanations are logically sound"; the attribution check marks these four restatements partial against Jacovi & Goldberg. Once plausibility means correctness, it can no longer be told apart from the validity-based definitions of cluster 4, such as [Xu et al. 2024](./#p=xu2024faithful)'s "’faithful’ if both the answer and the process are correct and logical".

**Lanham et al.'s tests become per-trace verdicts.** [Lanham et al. 2023](./#p=lanham2023measuring) report early answering and adding mistakes as task-level measures of post-hoc reasoning, read them with a hedge ("the model is likely not ignoring the CoT") and state that "Reasoning not being post-hoc does not guarantee faithfulness, nor does being post-hoc exclude faithfulness." Borrowers apply the tests to single traces: [Tanneru et al. 2024](./#p=tanneru2024hardness) compute the area over the curve per instance, and [Tutek et al. 2025](./#p=tutek2025measuring) write "If the prediction of the model changes, the CoT is considered faithful"; the check marks these restatements adapted and supported. The filler-tokens test changes its question: Lanham et al. ask whether filler accounts for CoT's accuracy gain ("If the filler tokens provide a significant performance improvement, then the CoT may be unfaithful"), while [Zaman & Srivastava 2025b](./#p=zaman2025chainofthought) call a CoT "unfaithful if this corruption does not change the original prediction", and the check marks this partial. Nine rows in cluster 2 cite Lanham et al., and the check marks 4 supported, 3 partial and 2 other-claim, the last five because the cue evidence or cue test they credit to Lanham et al. is not in that paper, which runs no cue tests.

**Cue tests: from counterfactual simulatability to verbalization rates.** [Turpin et al. 2023](./#p=turpin2023language) framed unfaithfulness predictively, as reasoning that can be "misleading about how the system will make predictions on other examples (in which case we also call it unfaithful)", and measured it by accuracy drops and by prediction pairs that change when the biasing feature is added. [Chen et al. 2025](./#p=chen2025reasoning) made verbalization itself the score and required both mention and dependence, and [Chua & Evans 2025](./#p=chua2025deepseek) required the CoT to "describe the switching effect of the cue". Downstream the requirement splits: [Meek et al. 2025](./#p=meek2025measuring) score "the presence of the given cue in a model’s reasoning trace", [Little 2026](./#p=little2026length) calls a chain faithful "when an LLM monitor can tell from it that the hint influenced the answer" (the check marks Little's restatement of Chen et al. partial, since Chen et al. score an abrupt switch to the hint answer as not verbalized), and [Walden & Wanner 2026](./#p=walden2026reasoning) turn Chen et al.'s score into an "intent-based honesty score". [Young 2026a](./#p=young2026measuring) concludes that the resulting numbers form "a family of related constructs whose values depend critically on the measurement instrument".

**Simulatability becomes a faithfulness score.** Forward simulation (Doshi-Velez & Kim 2017, outside the map) leads to [Hase et al. 2020](./#p=hase2020leakageadjusted)'s leakage-adjusted simulatability, which [Wang et al. 2023](./#p=wang2023scott), [Wang et al. 2022](./#p=wang2023pinto), [Paul et al. 2024](./#p=paul2024making) and [Chen et al. 2022](./#p=chen2022rev) use as the faithfulness of rationales ("To evaluate the faithfulness of the rationales generated by the student, we use LAS"). [Chen et al. 2023](./#p=pmlr-v235-chen24bl) place simulatability under faithfulness, "a special case of faithfulness, which requires the output predictor to be humans", and [Chen et al. 2025](./#p=chen2025reasoning) call it "a stricter form of faithfulness". [Mayne et al. 2026](./#p=mayne2026positivecasefaithfulnessllm)'s normalized simulatability gain is adopted by [Pres et al. 2026b](./#p=pres2026selfctrl), [Luo et al. 2026](./#p=luo2026removal) and [Lewis-Lim et al. 2026](./#p=lewislim2026efficient).

**Self-consistency and "parametric faithfulness".** [Parcalabescu & Frank 2023](./#p=parcalabescu2023measuring) argue that input- and CoT-perturbation tests measure self-consistency, "a necessary, but not sufficient test for faithfulness", and [Tutek et al. 2025](./#p=tutek2025measuring) use the argument to motivate parametric faithfulness, an intervention on the weights (unlearning a reasoning step), which [Zaman & Srivastava 2025b](./#p=zaman2025chainofthought) and [Sun et al. 2026](./#p=sun2026investigating) adopt. [Hong et al. 2026](./#p=hong2026making) keep both words but change both meanings: the check marks their "parametric faithfulness" partial against Chen et al., Hase & Potts and Zaman & Srivastava, and their sampling-based self-consistency partial against Parcalabescu & Frank and Tutek et al.

**Introspection: privileged access after Binder et al.** [Binder et al. 2024](./#p=binder2024looking) define introspection as the ability "to access facts about themselves that cannot be derived (logically or inductively) from their training data alone" and test it by cross-prediction; 10 papers borrow from them, and the check marks 7 of the 12 borrowings supported, 4 adapted and 1 other-claim. The reference model changes along the way: [Song et al. 2025](./#p=song2025language) compare against "another model with nearly identical internal knowledge", [Guo et al. 2026](./#p=guo2026introspective) claim that "a model can model its own behavior better than its training target", and [Zeng et al. 2026](./#p=zeng2026evaluating) define self-modeling by agreement alone and keep the cross-model comparison as a separate test of introspection. [Betley et al. 2025](./#p=betley2025tell)'s "behavioral self-awareness" is adopted by [Kutsyk & Zieliński 2026](./#p=kutsyk2026revealing), [Plunkett et al. 2025](./#p=plunkett2025self) and [Shenoy et al. 2026](./#p=shenoy2026introspection).

**Honesty: belief-relative lies from MASK, confessions from Joglekar et al.** Ren et al. 2025 supply the belief-relative test to [Anthropic 2026](./#p=anthropic2026opus55), [Blandfort & Pawar 2026](./#p=blandfort2026strangers), [Chang et al. 2026](./#p=chang2026catalyzing), [Tang & Khanna 2026](./#p=tang2026unlearning) and [Wang et al. 2025b](./#p=wang2025evaluating). [Joglekar et al. 2025](./#p=joglekar2025training)'s confessions are borrowed by four papers, and the check marks [Anonymous 2026 (honesty adapters)](./#p=anon2026traininghonesty)'s description of a confession as a field that "each assistant response also carries" partial, because Joglekar et al. request the confession as a separate output after the answer.

### Tensions

1. **Is an answer that survives a corrupted chain a sign of unfaithfulness?** [Lanham et al. 2023](./#p=lanham2023measuring) read an unchanged answer after an inserted mistake as evidence of post-hoc reasoning, with a hedge ("the model is likely not ignoring the CoT"), and do not treat post-hoc reasoning as unfaithful: "Reasoning not being post-hoc does not guarantee faithfulness, nor does being post-hoc exclude faithfulness." Rows that apply the test to single traces drop the hedge: [Tutek et al. 2025](./#p=tutek2025measuring) write "If the prediction of the model changes, the CoT is considered faithful", and [Akter et al. 2025](./#p=akter2025causal) require that "A faithful model, upon processing the logically inconsistent trace, should change its prediction or at least become less certain." [Cox et al. 2026](./#p=cox2026posthoc) accept the verdict while noting its cost: answering correctly despite a flawed scratchpad "is perhaps the preferred behavior, but it would constitute unfaithful reasoning". [Fareed 2026](./#p=fareed2026plan) rejects it: "corruption cannot distinguish unfaithfulness from justified correction, since ignoring a broken chain is good behavior". [Jia & Mu 2026](./#p=jia2026decorative) separate silent bypass from self-correction and call a high bypass rate "consistent with faithful reasoning" in the computation-to-CoT sense, while [Han et al. 2026b](./#p=han2026rfeval)'s stance consistency accepts a change of stance only when the output identifies the flaw, so that silent correction counts as unfaithful ("unfaithful-correct cases, such as silent corrections").

2. **Is an unverbalized but influential cue unfaithfulness or incompleteness?** For cluster 2 it is the paradigm case: "a CoT that does not verbalize the hint while using the hint is clearly unfaithful" ([Chen et al. 2025](./#p=chen2025reasoning); likewise [Turpin et al. 2023](./#p=turpin2023language), [Chua & Evans 2025](./#p=chua2025deepseek), [Arcuschin et al. 2026](./#p=arcuschin2026biases)). [Zaman & Srivastava 2025b](./#p=zaman2025chainofthought) answer that the test "confuses unfaithfulness with incompleteness, the lossy compression needed to turn distributed transformer computation into a linear natural language narrative", that it is "a test of verbalized sensitivity to a known intervention" which "is not the same as faithfulness", and that with more samples the probability of a hint-verbalizing CoT "increases to up to 90% in some settings"; they call the analysis "too strong for drawing conclusions about CoT faithfulness" and do not claim that the flagged traces are faithful. [Anonymous 2026 (one draw)](./#p=anon2026one) ("A trace that omits the cue may be incomplete rather than unfaithful") and [Nguyen et al. 2026b](./#p=nguyen2026generalization) ("cue acknowledgment does not establish faithfulness in the conventional sense") take the same side.

3. **Which cue cases count, and is mention enough?** An unchanged answer is faithful for [Turpin et al. 2023](./#p=turpin2023language), whose models must acknowledge biases or "give predictions unaffected by bias", and for [Turpin et al. 2025](./#p=turpin2025teaching): "If the model’s answer does not change between the prompts, we say it is faithful." [Chua & Evans 2025](./#p=chua2025deepseek) and [Chen et al. 2025](./#p=chen2025reasoning) score only switches ("We only test for faithfulness on the switched examples"), while [Meek et al. 2025](./#p=meek2025measuring) also score non-switches and count a model that does not discuss the cue as "not being faithful". Mention alone suffices for [Meek et al. 2025](./#p=meek2025measuring) and [Nguyen et al. 2026b](./#p=nguyen2026generalization); [Chen et al. 2025](./#p=chen2025reasoning), [egan 2026](./#p=egan2026hint) and [Anonymous 2026 (one draw)](./#p=anon2026one) also require that the CoT present the cue as a reason, and [Young 2026a](./#p=young2026measuring) shows that the two requirements are different constructs ("mention" vs "dependence").

4. **Is agreement between reasoning and answer faithfulness?** Cluster 4 says yes: "we use the term faithfulness to mean whether the model’s final answer is consistent with its generated chain-of-thought reasoning" ([Zhao et al. 2026](./#p=zhao2026robustness); also [Li et al. 2024](./#p=li2025better), [Cahlik et al. 2025](./#p=cahlik2025reasoninggrounded)), and systems whose answer is computed from the chain claim faithfulness by construction ([Lyu et al. 2023](./#p=lyu2023faithful); [Pan et al. 2023](./#p=pan2023logiclm); [Tafjord et al. 2022](./#p=tafjord2022entailer)). [Turpin et al. 2023](./#p=turpin2023language) call reasoning that is "coherent and consistent with its prediction" merely plausible, and possibly unfaithful, [Somov et al. 2026](./#p=somov2026breaking) write that "self-consistency alone does not establish causal reliance", and [Emmons et al. 2025b](./#p=emmons2025pragmatic) list "thought-response consistency" as distinct from faithfulness. [Lyu et al. 2023](./#p=lyu2023faithful) concede the point for unconstrained chains ("this still does not imply that the reasoning chain is a faithful explanation"), and [Zhao et al. 2026](./#p=zhao2026robustness) call their own measure weaker than "the stronger notion of explanation faithfulness in the interpretability literature".

5. **Behavioral tests or internals?** Clusters 2, 3 and 6 judge faithfulness from inputs and outputs, and [Arcuschin et al. 2025](./#p=arcuschin2025chain) ("behavioural faithfulness") and [Malladi & Srivastava 2026](./#p=malladi2026directly) ("behavioral faithfulness") name their notions accordingly. [Parcalabescu & Frank 2023](./#p=parcalabescu2023measuring) argue that input- and CoT-perturbation tests measure self-consistency, "a necessary, but not sufficient test for faithfulness". [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring) make a narrower claim, that "intervention-based proxies for measuring causality are insufficient measures for faithfulness", attribute the broader self-consistency argument to Parcalabescu & Frank, and add that "causal faithfulness metrics are still valuable and worth measuring". [Anonymous 2026 (GRACE)](./#p=anon2026grace) write that behavioral tests "cannot separate a model whose answer is supported by a different mechanism than its CoT suggests from one whose answer is simply robust to perturbation", and [Han et al. 2026b](./#p=han2026rfeval) that apparent unfaithfulness "is not a sufficient condition for unfaithfulness in the underlying process". [Macar et al. 2025](./#p=macar2025thought) add the converse case, that CoT text "can causally impact behavior" even when it does not explain model internals.

6. **May correctness enter the definition?** Four cluster 4 definitions tie faithfulness to correct reasoning or answers: [Xu et al. 2024](./#p=xu2024faithful) ("’unfaithful’ if the answer is correct but the process is not"), [Wang et al. 2025c](./#p=wang2025enhancing) ("unfaithful trajectories that guess the correct option after a faulty chain of thought"), [Li et al. 2024](./#p=li2025better), who compare the CoT with a gold rationale, and [Mohammadi et al. 2025](./#p=mohammadi2025evaluating), who check whether the reasoning "logically entails the reference explanation". Three papers exclude correctness: [Dehghanighobadi et al. 2025](./#p=dehghanighobadi2025can) hold that "the evaluation of faithfulness should not involve human-provided gold labels", [Hong et al. 2026](./#p=hong2026making) require agreement "regardless of whether the answer is correct, since CIA measures faithfulness rather than correctness", and [Shen et al. 2026](./#p=shen2026detecting) object to correctness-based indicators because "reasoning correctness and faithfulness are not equivalent". On the stricter reading a trace that accurately reports flawed reasoning is faithful, while the correctness-based definitions count it as unfaithful whenever the answer happens to be right.

7. **Is faithfulness honesty?** [Turpin et al. 2023](./#p=turpin2023language) tie the two conditionally: if models can recognize the biasing features' influence, "unfaithful CoT explanations may be a form of model dishonesty, as opposed to a lack of capability". [Walden & Wanner 2026](./#p=walden2026reasoning) adopt "a lenient notion of honesty" under which a CoT is honest when it states an intended or actual use of the hint, and note that the stated use "may or may not correspond" to how the hint is used "in a mechanistic sense", and [Sherburn et al. 2024](./#p=sherburn2024language) frame self-explanation the same way ("This paper focuses on honesty"). [Gur-Arieh et al. 2026](./#p=gurarieh2026faithfulness) reject this reading for step faithfulness, under which "the step would be considered faithful unless the model knowingly and deceptively lied", and adopt the mechanistic one. [Anonymous 2026 (introspective oversight)](./#p=anon2026trainingoversight) and [Joglekar et al. 2025](./#p=joglekar2025training) separate honesty from accuracy: "models can sincerely describe their actions incorrectly", and Joglekar et al. claim only that honest confessions are easy to produce, not accurate ones ("This is not to say that producing accurate confessions is an easy task, only honest ones").

8. **What makes a statement a lie or deception: a belief, an intent or a mismatch with action?** [Wang et al. 2025b](./#p=wang2025evaluating) call a statement a lie if the model "believes it to be false", requiring neither "Deceptive intent" nor "Active awareness of falsehood at generation time", and note that this "differs somewhat from the colloquial notion of lying"; [Tang & Khanna 2026](./#p=tang2026unlearning) call a claim the model does not believe deception "whether or not an operator instruction asked for it", while holding that "Not all context-dependent non-literal behavior is deception", such as roleplay or fiction. [Anthropic 2026](./#p=anthropic2026opus55)'s white-box audit requires knowledge, a model that "Holds a reason or judgment that contradicts what it presents, or knowingly leaves out something the reader needs to know". [Taufeeque et al. 2026](./#p=taufeeque2026obfuscation) define a deceptive response with no statement at all, as "code specifically designed to pass the visible test cases without solving the general problem", and [Bu et al. 2026](./#p=bu2026spadebench) as "a spontaneous inconsistency between an agent’s declared plan (what it says it will do) and its executed action (what it actually does)". Monitor rubrics call claiming an action not taken "lying to the user" ([Anonymous 2026 (implementation)](./#p=anon2026implementation); [Chidambaram et al. 2026](./#p=chidambaram2026corrupted)), while [Dietz et al. 2026](./#p=dietz2026split) note that "fabrication may occur without the model representing the output as false" and [Shiromani & Richter 2026](./#p=shiromani2026false) separate action-faithfulness from "whether the stated intent is honest".

9. **Is an accurate self-report enough?** [Zeng et al. 2026](./#p=zeng2026evaluating) count self-modeling as successful "whenever its self-reports agree with its observed input-output behavior", whether the report draws "on internal access, on cues in the prompt, or on general knowledge of how language models behave". [Atkinson et al. 2026](./#p=atkinson2026identifying) call such agreement faithfulness and require grounding as well ("Grounding requires reporting the true cause"), so that an accurate report through an ungrounded mechanism is "a correct confabulator". [Binder et al. 2024](./#p=binder2024looking) require that a stronger model given the reporter's training data not report the same fact, and [Anonymous 2026 (introspection routes)](./#p=anon2026introspection) write that "a self-report can be accurate without being introspective". Under the privileged-access test the evidence divides: [Blandfort & Pawar 2026](./#p=blandfort2026strangers) find that "Verbal testimony describes other models about as well as the self", while [Li et al. 2025b](./#p=li2025training) find that "the target model is better explained by itself than by other explainer models".
