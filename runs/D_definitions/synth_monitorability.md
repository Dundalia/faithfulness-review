The extraction holds 328 definitions in the monitorability family, taken from 82 of the 217 papers. By kind, 92 are informal, 64 operational, 59 failure-mode, 42 criterion, 38 taxonomy and 33 formal; by stance, 196 are proposed, 104 adopted and 28 adapted. Only 108 entries, from 40 papers, have a term that names monitorability or a monitorable object; the remaining 220 define companion notions. The picture in one sentence: most definitions make monitorability a relation between a model and a reader, namely whether a given monitor can predict a specified property of the model's behaviour from what the model exposes ([Guan et al. 2025](./#p=guan2025monitoring), [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring)), while a second group makes it a property of the trace alone, its faithful and complete verbalization of what drove the answer, often citing the same sources for that stronger reading. The nine clusters below place every entry in exactly one cluster. The first two belong together: the first holds what monitorability is in the predictive sense, the second the scores and evaluation designs that measure it, and they are kept apart because the literature cites them separately.

### Monitorability as a monitor's ability to predict a target property

30 entries from 22 papers (21 informal, 6 criterion, 2 formal, 1 taxonomy; 15 adopted, 13 proposed, 2 adapted).

Monitorability here is relative to a property and to a reader. A model is monitorable for property $Y$ by monitor $M$ to the degree that $M$, reading what the model exposes (CoT, actions or, in some entries, activations), predicts $Y$. The cluster's defining move is negative: the CoT need not reproduce the model's internal computation. Several entries say this explicitly and set the definition against faithfulness (see the [Faithfulness](#faithfulness) section).

> We develop evaluations for monitorability, which we define as a monitor’s ability to accurately predict a specified property of an agent’s actions, e.g. whether the model is acting or will act harmfully.
>
> — [Guan et al. 2025](./#p=guan2025monitoring), §2

**Formal versions.**
- [Anonymous 2026 (implementation)](./#p=anon2026implementation), Informal Def. 1: property $P$ of actor $A$ is monitorable on task distribution $D_t$ with respect to monitor $M$ if $P(A,t,\pi)\approx M(\pi)$ with high probability over $t\sim D_t$, $\pi\sim A(t)$. Here $P$ may depend on the actor and the task, not only on the transcript $\pi$.
- [Anwar et al. 2026](./#p=anwar2026analyzing), Def. 3.6 and Props. 3.7, 3.10: the uplift from reading the CoT $Z$ is $\delta := \alpha_{\text{CoT}}-\alpha_{\text{Prompt}}$, the accuracy gain of the Bayes-optimal CoT monitor over the Bayes-optimal prompt-only monitor. It is bounded by $\sqrt{2\,I(Y;Z\mid X)}$, and $\delta>0$ requires $I(O;Z\mid X)>0$, where $O$ is the output. App. F.2 adds that an attribute is easier to monitor when $H(Y\mid X,Z)$ is small.
- [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring) connects the definition to faithfulness by redefining the latter. A latent $z$ is faithful with respect to $v$ if $z$ is a sufficient statistic for $v$, and a monitor that predicts $v$ from $z$ shows that $z$ is faithful with respect to $v$ and legible to it (see Attribution check, item 2).

### Scoring monitorability: metrics and evaluation designs

55 entries from 31 papers (27 operational, 13 formal, 12 taxonomy, 2 informal, 1 criterion; 36 proposed, 12 adopted, 7 adapted).

This cluster operationalizes the previous one. Monitorability becomes the agreement between a monitor's verdict $Z$ and a ground-truth label for the target property, summarized by a statistic. The entries differ in three choices: how the label is produced (an input intervention such as a hint, a known solution process, or a verifiable outcome), which statistic is used (g-mean², AUROC, binned MSE, detection or catch rate), and the regime (default or adversarial). Where the label comes from an intervention, the score is tied to a counterfactual contrast between the answer with and without the intervention (see the [Counterfactual](#counterfactual) section). [Hase & Potts 2026](./#p=hase2026counterfactual) states the link directly, treating monitoring as counterfactual simulation.

> A natural choice is the geometric mean of sensitivity (true positive rate) and specificity (true negative rate), $\text{g-mean}=\sqrt{\mathrm{TPR}\cdot\mathrm{TNR}}$ [25]. We square this quantity to more sharply penalize failures, yielding a monitorability measure which is both more conservative and more immediately interpretable
>
> — [Guan et al. 2025](./#p=guan2025monitoring), §2.1

**Formal versions.**
- [Guan et al. 2025](./#p=guan2025monitoring): $\text{g-mean}^2=\mathrm{TPR}\cdot\mathrm{TNR}$; a random monitor scores 0.25. For intervention evaluations, the true positive rate is corrected for answers the intervention did not cause:
  $$\mathrm{TPR}_{mc}=\min\Big\{1,\ \frac{\Pr(Z=1\mid X=1,Y=1)}{R_{\text{effect}}}\Big\},\qquad R_{\text{effect}}=\frac{\mathrm{TE}}{\Pr(Y=1\mid X=1)},\qquad \text{g-mean}^2_{\text{int}}=\mathrm{TPR}_{mc}\sqrt{\mathrm{TNR}_{X=1}\,\mathrm{TNR}_{X=0}}.$$
- [Anonymous 2026 (improving monitorability)](./#p=anon2026improving): causal recall $r_c:=\Pr(Z(1)=1\mid U=1)$ with $U:=\mathbb{1}\{Y(1)=1,\,Y(0)=0\}$. With $R=(p_1-p_0)/p_1$, the observable bounds are $\max\{0,(\mathrm{TPR}_{ob}+R-1)/R\}\le r_c\le\min\{1,\mathrm{TPR}_{ob}/R\}$. The upper end is Guan's $\mathrm{TPR}_{mc}$, which therefore bounds causal recall from above and does not identify it (Theorem 1).
- [Wang et al. 2026](./#p=wang2026monitorbench): $\min\{\mathbb{E}[Z\mid X=1,Y=1]/R_{\text{effect}},\,1\}$, the same form applied to flag rates on outcome and process tasks.
- [Bürger et al. 2026](./#p=burger2026chain): $M_0=\min(1,\pi_Z/R)$ with $R=\max(0,p_{11}-p_{01})/p_{11}$. Here $\pi_Z$ is the rate at which the CoT *articulates* the attribute, not a monitor's flag rate. $M_1$ is the same score restricted to cases where the CoT is necessary ($p_{10}<0.01$).
- [Anonymous 2026 (implementation)](./#p=anon2026implementation): $\hat\mu=s\big(M(\pi),\hat P(e(A),t_{\hat P},\pi)\big)\in[0,1]$, the agreement of monitor score and a misbehaviour proxy on elicited transcripts. The default statistic is the binned MSE $\mathrm{BMSE}(X,Y)=\operatorname{mean}_b\,\mathbb{E}[(X-Y)^2\mid Y\in b]$ over non-empty bins $[0,\tfrac12]$ and $(\tfrac12,1]$ (lower is more monitorable). The paired variant is $s(\Delta M,\Delta\hat P)$.
- [Yang et al. 2025](./#p=yang2025investigating): false negatives $\Pr(M=0\mid Y_{\text{cue}}=1)$ and false positives $\Pr(M=1\mid Y_{\text{cue}}=0)$, estimated as $\mathrm{EEMR}=(1-\mathrm{MFR})\cdot\mathrm{CIR}_a$ and $\mathrm{OSM}=\mathrm{MFR}\cdot\mathrm{AKR}_a$.
- [Hase & Potts 2026](./#p=hase2026counterfactual) uses the unsquared $\sqrt{\mathrm{TPR}\cdot(1-\mathrm{FPR})}$; [MacDermott et al. 2025](./#p=macdermott2025reasoning) trains on a reward of $+1$ when the monitor predicts the latent correctly and $-1$ otherwise, reweighted by inverse class frequency; [Anwar et al. 2026](./#p=anwar2026analyzing) trains on $I(O;Z\mid X)$ as a label-free proxy for $I(Y;Z\mid X)$.

### Monitorability as faithful and complete verbalization

38 entries from 23 papers (14 criterion, 11 informal, 6 operational, 3 taxonomy, 2 failure-mode, 2 formal; 24 proposed, 12 adopted, 2 adapted).

Here monitorability is a property of the trace itself. The CoT must state the factors and steps that produced the answer, and monitoring presupposes faithfulness (see the [Faithfulness](#faithfulness) section, which these definitions invoke explicitly). Within the cluster, the entries differ on how much must be verbalized. Some require faithfulness plus completeness ([Meek et al. 2025](./#p=meek2025measuring): faithfulness and verbosity). Some require faithfulness plus legibility ([Xiong et al. 2026](./#p=xiong2026monitorability), [Google DeepMind 2026](./#p=google2026gemini4argon)). Others require only the monitored property or the key influences ([Anonymous 2026 (CoT reliance)](./#p=anon2026chainofthought), [Bürger et al. 2026](./#p=burger2026chain), [Kaufmann et al. 2026](./#p=kaufmann2026aligned)). Two entries locate monitorability in the model alone, independent of any monitor ([Xiong et al. 2026](./#p=xiong2026monitorability), [Yang et al. 2025](./#p=yang2025investigating)).

> This visibility could help us spot unsafe or misaligned behavior (monitorability), but only if the CoT is transparent about its internal reasoning (faithfulness).
>
> — [Meek et al. 2025](./#p=meek2025measuring), Abstract

**Formal versions.**
- [Meek et al. 2025](./#p=meek2025measuring): monitorability $=\tfrac12(F+V)$. $F$ is the cue-acknowledgment rate and $V$ the fraction of LLM-panel causal factors present in the uncued CoT. The paper notes that a weighted mean, geometric mean or minimum could replace the average.
- [Bürger et al. 2026](./#p=burger2026chain), Eq. 3: a monitorable CoT satisfies $\pi_Z\ge\mathrm{KR}$, where $\pi_Z$ is the articulation rate and $\mathrm{KR}$ the unobservable key-influence rate. Since $R\le\mathrm{KR}$, the observable test is $\pi_Z\ge R$.
- [Kaufmann et al. 2026](./#p=kaufmann2026aligned), Defs. C.1–C.2: a CoT on input $I$ is monitorable for monitor $M$ and task $T$ iff $f^M_T\big(M_{\text{sem}}(\mathrm{CoT})\big)=f^M_T\big(\pi_{\text{sem}}(\mathrm{CoT})\big)$. That is, the monitor classifies the text as it would under the model's own semantics. The monitorability of a policy is $\mathbb{E}_{I\sim\rho}\,\mathbb{1}\big[f^M_T(M_{\text{sem}}(\mathrm{CoT}_I))=f^M_T(\pi_{\text{sem}}(\mathrm{CoT}_I))\big]$.

### Necessity and causal dependence of the CoT

25 entries from 16 papers (10 criterion, 5 informal, 4 operational, 3 failure-mode, 2 taxonomy, 1 formal; 16 proposed, 8 adopted, 1 adapted).

These entries define the condition under which a CoT is expected to be monitorable, not monitorability itself. The first version is necessity, a condition on the task: the model cannot succeed without the CoT. [Korbak et al. 2025](./#p=korbak2025chain) separates it from the mere propensity to think out loud. The second version is causal dependence, a condition on the trace: the answer must depend on what the CoT says. [Skaf et al. 2025](./#p=skaf2025large)'s load-bearing CoT, [Jia & Mu 2026](./#p=jia2026decorative) and [Anonymous 2026 (CRISP)](./#p=anon2026crisp) take this route. [Fareed 2026](./#p=fareed2026plan) applies it to agents' stated plans.

> Necessity: We say CoT is necessary for a task if the model cannot succeed without it. This condition formalizes the requirement that a model must “think out loud.”
>
> — [Emmons et al. 2025 (necessity)](./#p=emmons2025chain), §2

**Formal versions.** [Bürger et al. 2026](./#p=burger2026chain), Def. 1: the CoT is necessary for the target answer if $p_{10}<0.01$, the no-CoT probability of that answer. Its hierarchy then runs Level 0 (not necessary), Level 1 (necessary for the answer), Level 2 (necessary to process property $P$) and Level 3.$m$ ($m$ consecutive forward passes dedicated to $P$). [Anonymous 2026 (CoT reliance)](./#p=anon2026chainofthought) replaces the threshold with a graded reliance $\mathrm{acc}_{\text{CoT}}-\mathrm{acc}_{\text{no-CoT}}$ per (model, task) pair. [Fareed 2026](./#p=fareed2026plan)'s binding rate is the frequency with which the realized irreversible write matches the seeded plan; zero variance across seeds marks a causally inert plan.

### Legibility and coverage: a reader can follow, check or reconstruct the reasoning

52 entries from 19 papers (14 operational, 12 informal, 10 failure-mode, 7 formal, 5 taxonomy, 4 criterion; 31 proposed, 13 adopted, 8 adapted).

Legibility is a relation between the trace and a named reader. The reader is a time-limited human or a verifier in [Kirchner et al. 2024](./#p=kirchner2024proververifier), a human with the user's language in [Emmons et al. 2025 (pragmatic)](./#p=emmons2025pragmatic), another model in [Roytburg et al. 2026](./#p=roytburg2026measuring) (a weaker one), [Jiao et al. 2026](./#p=jiao2026tandem) (the frozen pre-RL junior) and [Sullivan & Koller 2026](./#p=sullivan2026language) (instruct models, and peers trained the same way), and the monitor itself in [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring). Coverage and its relatives (transfer utility, trace legibility, sufficiency of reasoning, the information-sufficiency gate in [Mao et al. 2026](./#p=mao2026taming)) ask whether that reader can reach the answer from the trace. The failure modes are illegibility, language drift and compression. [Kirchner et al. 2024](./#p=kirchner2024proververifier) and [Jose 2025](./#p=jose2025reasoning) define legibility as independent of how the model actually reasoned.

> One way to increase confidence in the outputs of Large Language Models (LLMs) is to support them with reasoning that is clear and easy to check — a property we call legibility.
>
> — [Kirchner et al. 2024](./#p=kirchner2024proververifier), Abstract

**Formal versions.**
- [Kirchner et al. 2024](./#p=kirchner2024proververifier), §3.1: prover $\pi$ is verifiable to $v$ iff completeness holds, $\forall x:\ \mathrm{correct}(x,\pi(x))=v(x,\pi(x))=1$, and soundness holds, $\forall\pi'\in\mathcal H,\ \forall x:\ \mathrm{correct}(x,\pi'(x))=0\Rightarrow v(x,\pi'(x))=0$. [Kim & Lee 2026](./#p=kim2026mitigating) drops correctness from completeness: correct $\Rightarrow v(x,\pi(x))=1$.
- [Sullivan & Koller 2026](./#p=sullivan2026language), Def. 1: $LD(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_x\big[\mathrm{KL}\big(P^{(HL)}(\cdot\mid x)\,\big\Vert\,\pi(\cdot\mid x)\big)\big]$ over prompts from the human-language marginal.
- [Jiao et al. 2026](./#p=jiao2026tandem): junior legibility is the junior's mean per-token cross-entropy on the senior's CoT, and the overlap $\alpha=\sum_v\min\big(p_{\text{sen}}(v\mid\cdot),\,p_{\text{jun}}(v\mid\cdot)\big)$ averaged over positions.
- [Roytburg et al. 2026](./#p=roytburg2026measuring): $\mathrm{FOTU}(T)$ is the mean over prefix bins $x$ of $\mu_T(x)$, the weak model's accuracy given $x\%$ of the trace. SOTU is the normalized entropy of the bin where the weak model first answers correctly, and the regression rate is the fraction of consecutive prefixes where weak-model correctness drops.
- [Yu et al. 2026](./#p=yu2026outcome): reasoning is sufficient if $p_\theta(\cdot\mid q,t')\approx p_\theta(\cdot\mid t')$ for the answer-stripped CoT $t'$.

### Encoded reasoning and steganography

35 entries from 17 papers (19 failure-mode, 7 informal, 4 operational, 3 criterion, 1 taxonomy, 1 formal; 21 proposed, 13 adopted, 1 adapted).

The CoT carries information that the model uses but a reader cannot recover. The information may be hidden in word choice or syntax (lexical schemes), in patterns of reasoning steps ([Zhou & May 2026](./#p=zhou2026conceptual)), or in a channel between models ([Mathew et al. 2024](./#p=mathew2025hidden); [Skaf et al. 2025](./#p=skaf2025large) separates this steganographic communication from steganographic reasoning). The entries agree that the hidden content must be used by the model: [Skaf et al. 2025](./#p=skaf2025large) requires load-bearing tokens, and [Roger 2025](./#p=anthropic2025paraphrases) tests use by paraphrase distillation. They disagree on who the content is hidden from (see Tensions).

> Encoded reasoning: The benefit from CoT may be attained by LLMs encoding the relevant information in the generated reasoning in a way that is not understandable to human readers (a form of steganography).
>
> — [Lanham et al. 2023](./#p=lanham2023measuring), §1

**Formal versions.** [Roger & Greenblatt 2023](./#p=roger2023preventing) measures the capacity of the encoder–paraphraser–decoder channel as $I(M,\hat M)=H(M)-H(M\mid\hat M)$. It estimates this from the exact-decode rate $f$ on $n$-bit payloads as $C=n-f\log_2\frac1f-(1-f)\log_2\frac{2^n-1}{1-f}$. [Skaf et al. 2025](./#p=skaf2025large): a trace is steganographic with respect to a monitor if (a) it encodes harmful planning in load-bearing tokens and (b) the monitor can neither decode the encodings nor detect their presence. If the monitor detects the encodings without decoding them, the CoT is overtly encoded instead.

### Obfuscation, evasion and controllability

47 entries from 29 papers (24 failure-mode, 6 operational, 6 taxonomy, 5 informal, 3 formal, 3 criterion; 26 proposed, 17 adopted, 4 adapted).

In this cluster the misbehaviour continues while the trace stops revealing it to the monitor. The mechanisms named are omission, misleading framing, content-preserving rephrasing that fools only the LLM monitor ([Schulz 2026](./#p=schulz2026monitor)), changed internal representations that defeat a probe ([Taufeeque et al. 2026](./#p=taufeeque2026obfuscation)), and injected plans that the model adopts as its own ([Chidambaram et al. 2026](./#p=chidambaram2026corrupted)). The usual cause is optimization pressure, whether direct (a CoT monitor in the reward) or indirect (output-only supervision in [Drori et al. 2025](./#p=drori2025output), consistency training in [Imran et al. 2026](./#p=imran2026consistency)). Monitor awareness and instructions are also named. CoT controllability, the ability to shape one's own CoT under instruction, is treated as a precursor capability.

> such an objective could cause agents to learn to hide their intent within the CoT while continuing to exhibit misaligned behavior, a phenomenon we will call obfuscated reward hacking.
>
> — [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring), §1

**Formal versions.** [Drori et al. 2025](./#p=drori2025output), Defs. 3, 5, 6. With $M_{\text{out}}=m(p,o)$ and $M_{\text{CoT}}=m(p,c)$, feedback spillover is $\mathbb{E}_\theta[M_{\text{CoT}}]<\mathbb{E}_{\theta_{\text{control}}}[M_{\text{CoT}}]$. Spillover is conditional if $\nabla_{\text{CoT}}\mathbb{E}[M_{\text{out}}]\cdot\nabla_{\text{CoT}}\mathbb{E}[M_{\text{CoT}}]>0$ (safe-looking CoTs make safe-looking outputs likelier) and parametric if $\nabla_{\text{out}}\mathbb{E}[M_{\text{out}}]\cdot\nabla_{\text{CoT}}\mathbb{E}[M_{\text{CoT}}]>0$ (updates that make outputs look safe also make CoTs look safe). [Taufeeque et al. 2026](./#p=taufeeque2026obfuscation) separates obfuscated policies from obfuscated activations with the probe-score decomposition $\psi(y,\theta_0)$ versus $\psi(y,\theta_0)-\psi(y,\theta)$.

### Monitors, monitor scopes and oversight channels

38 entries from 27 papers (21 informal, 8 taxonomy, 4 formal, 3 operational, 1 criterion, 1 failure-mode; 26 proposed, 10 adopted, 2 adapted).

These entries define the reader. They cover what a CoT monitor is (an LLM classifier, a map to $[0,1]$, a suspicion score meant to track a property) and what it observes. The monitor scopes are CoT-only, action-only and full; [Algaba et al. 2026](./#p=algaba2026reasoning)'s observability ladder runs from the response through a self-summary and the trace to internal signals. They also cover how protocols chain monitors (trusted and bootstrapped monitoring) and channels in which the model reports on itself: confessions, self-incrimination, and monitorability disposition. [Emmons et al. 2025 (necessity)](./#p=emmons2025chain) adds automatability, the condition that the monitor can run at scale.

> A CoT monitor is an automated system that reads the CoT of a reasoning model and other relevant information and flags suspicious or potentially harmful interactions.

### Monitorability and legibility taxes

8 entries from 8 papers (8 informal; 4 adopted, 3 proposed, 1 adapted).

A tax is the cost paid to keep reasoning monitorable or legible. All eight entries are informal. They disagree about what the cost is: capability forgone by not optimizing the CoT ([Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring)), capability lost by using a smaller model at higher reasoning effort for the same compute ([Guan et al. 2025](./#p=guan2025monitoring)), monitorability lost to compression, which depends on the mechanism ([Li 2026](./#p=li2026monitorability)), the drop in exploit quality a model suffers when it evades the monitor ([Google DeepMind 2026](./#p=google2026gemini4argon)), or accuracy lost by training for checkability ([Kirchner et al. 2024](./#p=kirchner2024proververifier)). The section on Lineage traces how the term moved.

**Guan et al. 2025: the definition and the metric.** [Bürger et al. 2026](./#p=burger2026chain) quotes the definition verbatim: "We adopt the definition of Guan et al. (2026), who define monitorability as ”a monitor’s ability to accurately predict a specified property of an agent’s actions”". Other restatements change what is read and what is predicted. Guan's object is "an agent's actions", and the definition is meant to apply to any observation; the restatements narrow it to the CoT: "whether a monitor can infer particular properties of a model’s behavior from its CoT" ([OpenAI 2026](./#p=openai2026gpt6astra)), "whether a specific property of the model’s behaviour can be recovered from the CoT by an external monitor" ([Lewis-Lim et al. 2026](./#p=lewislim2026efficient)). The predicted property then becomes a "latent variable" ([Li 2026](./#p=li2026monitorability), via [MacDermott et al. 2025](./#p=macdermott2025reasoning)) and, in [Mao et al. 2026](./#p=mao2026taming), "balanced recoverability of the two-gate status". The g-mean² metric is adopted unchanged in 5 entries ([Duzan & Cooper Stickland 2026](./#p=duzan2026chainofthought), [Lewis-Lim et al. 2026](./#p=lewislim2026efficient), [Anonymous 2026 (CoT reliance)](./#p=anon2026chainofthought), [Xiong et al. 2026](./#p=xiong2026monitorability), [Mao et al. 2026](./#p=mao2026taming)) and adapted in 1 ([Anonymous 2026 (reward misspecification)](./#p=anon2026reward)). The three evaluation archetypes are adopted in 4 entries ([OpenAI 2026](./#p=openai2026gpt6astra), [Duzan & Cooper Stickland 2026](./#p=duzan2026chainofthought), [Anonymous 2026 (CoT reliance)](./#p=anon2026chainofthought), [Bürger et al. 2026](./#p=burger2026chain)) and adapted in 1 ([Wang et al. 2026](./#p=wang2026monitorbench)). Two adaptations change the meaning of the minimal-criterion score. [Wang et al. 2026](./#p=wang2026monitorbench) keeps the monitor's flag rate. [Bürger et al. 2026](./#p=burger2026chain) replaces it with the CoT's articulation rate, turning a monitor-relative score into a trace-relative one. [Anonymous 2026 (improving monitorability)](./#p=anon2026improving) adopts $\mathrm{TPR}_{mc}$ and shows that it only upper-bounds causal recall.

**Baker et al. 2025: one source, two readings.** Baker's definition ("the ability to monitor for specific properties of agents actions or outputs (monitorability), and not faithfulness in isolation, that is important") is passed on along two branches. In the predictive branch the property narrows to bad actions. [Emmons et al. 2025 (necessity)](./#p=emmons2025chain) adapts it to "if the reader can use the CoT to determine if the model is taking bad actions". [Anonymous 2026 (CoT reliance)](./#p=anon2026chainofthought) restates this via Emmons as "determine whether a model is taking an undesirable action based on reading its CoT". [Emmons et al. 2025 (pragmatic)](./#p=emmons2025pragmatic) cites both as "whether a monitor can use the CoT to determine if a model is taking dangerous actions". In the verbalization branch the property becomes the reasoning process. Examples are [Reddy et al. 2025](./#p=reddy2025principled) ("the ability to inspect and evaluate a model’s internal reasoning process through its generated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) traces"), [Little 2026](./#p=little2026length) ("whether the trace reveals what drove the answer"), [Haskins et al. 2026](./#p=haskins2026training) ("its CoT faithfully externalizes reasoning in a way that makes such detection possible") and [Occhipinti et al. 2026](./#p=occhipinti2026probing) ("Faithfulness is a prerequisite for CoT monitorability (Baker et al., 2025)"). Of the 28 Baker-citing entries, 6 fall in the predictive cluster and 8 in the verbalization cluster. Five of those 8 cite Korbak et al. 2025 alongside Baker. Baker's obfuscated reward hacking is the most-borrowed failure mode. Nine Baker-citing entries fall in the obfuscation cluster, under names such as obfuscated CoT ([Drori et al. 2025](./#p=drori2025output)), obfuscated misbehavior ([Cui et al. 2026](./#p=cui2026behavior)) and obfuscation under monitor penalty ([Zhou & Tao 2026](./#p=zhou2026nearzero)). [Skaf et al. 2025](./#p=skaf2025large) widens it to an umbrella term ("any reasoning which does not reveal harmful planning with respect to a monitor"), and [Schulz 2026](./#p=schulz2026monitor) narrows it against encoded reasoning.

**Korbak et al. 2025.** The paper's CoT-monitor definition is reused closely by [Anwar et al. 2026](./#p=anwar2026analyzing) ("LLM-based systems that analyze reasoning traces to detect when outputs may exhibit attributes of interest"), [Ferreira et al. 2025](./#p=ferreira2026truthful) and [Chidambaram et al. 2026](./#p=chidambaram2026corrupted). [Anonymous 2026 (introspective oversight)](./#p=anon2026trainingoversight) extends it to "verbalized reasoning that is causally relevant for generating responses". Its necessity argument passes to [Google DeepMind 2026](./#p=google2026gemini4argon) ("being able to recurse via CoT increases a model’s serial depth"). Eleven of the 18 Korbak-citing entries cite it for what monitorability is or requires, a definition the paper does not give (Attribution check, item 1).

**Emmons et al. 2025 (necessity).** The necessity condition ("the model cannot succeed without it") is adopted by [Schulz 2026](./#p=schulz2026monitor), [Anonymous 2026 (CoT reliance)](./#p=anon2026chainofthought) and [Emmons et al. 2025 (pragmatic)](./#p=emmons2025pragmatic) with little change. It then drifts in three ways. [Duzan & Cooper Stickland 2026](./#p=duzan2026chainofthought) renames it load-bearing ("when the model cannot reach its answer without working through it serially in its reasoning trace"), a word [Skaf et al. 2025](./#p=skaf2025large) uses for a different condition on the trace ("the tokens themselves convey information which the model uses to perform downstream behavior"). Duzan also attaches it to faithfulness ("Prior work suggests CoT is more faithful when the reasoning is load-bearing"). [Dhoot & Hao 2026](./#p=dhoot2026hintfollowing) names the finding the "necessity effect", a phrase that does not occur in the source. [Bürger et al. 2026](./#p=burger2026chain) refines the binary condition into levels, and [Anonymous 2026 (CoT reliance)](./#p=anon2026chainofthought) replaces it with a graded gap.

**Encoded reasoning.** [Lanham et al. 2023](./#p=lanham2023measuring) proposes the term ("not understandable to human readers (a form of steganography)"). [Roger & Greenblatt 2023](./#p=roger2023preventing) adopts it with credit to Lanham, and [Zhou & May 2026](./#p=zhou2026conceptual) later credits the term to Roger & Greenblatt ("a phenomenon termed encoded reasoning (Roger and Greenblatt, 2023)"). **Legibility.** [Kirchner et al. 2024](./#p=kirchner2024proververifier)'s legibility ("clear and easy to check") is defined through checkability. [Jiao et al. 2026](./#p=jiao2026tandem) restates that link, and [Kim & Lee 2026](./#p=kim2026mitigating) adapts checkability. [Guan et al. 2025](./#p=guan2025monitoring) narrows it to one trade: "The capability cost incurred from switching to a smaller model using the same amount of compute". [Chen et al. 2025](./#p=chen2025reasoning) does tie its faithfulness evaluation to monitoring. **Partially supported.** The proxy framing is Chen's and Korbak's, not Lanham's or Turpin's.

**Not settled.**
- [Jose 2025](./#p=jose2025reasoning) describes compressed reasoning as "dropping words and proper sentence form, mirroring patterns reported in OpenAI’s CoTs (Baker et al., 2025)". Baker has no record for it, and the word "compress" does not occur in its text. The patterns may appear in its figures, which were not inspected.
- [Lee et al. 2026](./#p=lee2026training) cites [Schoen et al. 2025](./#p=schoen2025stress) for "covert misbehavior". Schoen has no record for the term, and its text speaks of "covert action rates". The substance probably matches; the wording does not.
- [Skaf et al. 2025](./#p=skaf2025large) defines encoded reasoning after reference [6], which resolves to Greenblatt et al. 2024. That paper is not in the map, and the extraction note says the work described is Roger & Greenblatt 2023. The attribution was not checked against Greenblatt et al. 2024.
- [Mao et al. 2026](./#p=mao2026taming) calls obfuscation "an operational, non-intentional training outcome" (Guan; Drori; Haskins). Drori's spillover is unintended. Guan's obfuscation runs reward obfuscation deliberately, though Guan also mentions natural reductions from accidental CoT pressure. Haskins induces obfuscation by training on documents about monitoring. The sources are mixed, so this is not settled.

### Tensions

**Does monitorability require faithfulness?** One side says it does not, or requires only a weaker condition. Its definitions are [Guan et al. 2025](./#p=guan2025monitoring) ("does not require a model’s externalized reasoning to faithfully reflect its internal reasoning process"), [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring), [MacDermott et al. 2025](./#p=macdermott2025reasoning) ("even if that reasoning is unfaithful"), [Kaufmann et al. 2026](./#p=kaufmann2026aligned) and [Anonymous 2026 (CoT reliance)](./#p=anon2026chainofthought) ("monitorability remains an easier objective than faithfulness because of a weaker overall comprehensiveness requirement"). The other side requires it: [Meek et al. 2025](./#p=meek2025measuring) ("but only if the CoT is transparent about its internal reasoning (faithfulness)"), [Occhipinti et al. 2026](./#p=occhipinti2026probing), [Xiong et al. 2026](./#p=xiong2026monitorability), [Haskins et al. 2026](./#p=haskins2026training), [Bhupatiraju & Nyaupane 2026](./#p=bhupatiraju2026stated), [Anonymous 2026 (one draw)](./#p=anon2026one), [Jose 2025](./#p=jose2025reasoning), [Chen et al. 2025](./#p=chen2025reasoning), [Google DeepMind 2026](./#p=google2026gemini4argon) and [Golechha et al. 2026](./#p=golechha2026preliminary). A test case is a CoT that names the hint that changed the answer but gives a reconstructed account of how the answer was computed. For Guan and Baker it is monitorable for hint-reliance. For Xiong it is not ("faithfully reflect the model’s internal decision-making path"), nor for Bhupatiraju & Nyaupane ("reflects the computation that directly produces its answer"). Baker's property-relative faithfulness, a sufficient statistic for the monitored property, is the sense in which both sides could agree. However, the entries requiring faithfulness use either the reasoning-process sense or hint acknowledgment.

**Is monitorability a property of the model, or of the model and a reader?** Most definitions make it reader-relative: [Guan et al. 2025](./#p=guan2025monitoring), [Emmons et al. 2025 (necessity)](./#p=emmons2025chain) ("monitorable to a reader (human or automated)"), [Anonymous 2026 (improving monitorability)](./#p=anon2026improving) ("depends on both the task model and the monitoring setup"), [Anonymous 2026 (implementation)](./#p=anon2026implementation), [Kaufmann et al. 2026](./#p=kaufmann2026aligned) and [Algaba et al. 2026](./#p=algaba2026reasoning) ("any monitorability claim, including for faithfulness, should specify both"). [Emmons et al. 2025 (pragmatic)](./#p=emmons2025pragmatic) measures it for a human monitor as a lower bound on general monitorability. On the other side, [Xiong et al. 2026](./#p=xiong2026monitorability) makes it "independent of the monitor’s specific capabilities", even though its g-mean² is computed for one monitor. [Yang et al. 2025](./#p=yang2025investigating) separates being monitorable (verbalization) from being monitored correctly (monitor reliability). [Meek et al. 2025](./#p=meek2025measuring)'s score contains no monitor term at all. On the reader-relative definitions, a CoT read by two monitors of different strength has two monitorability values; on Xiong's, it has one.

**Must the CoT be causally upstream of the answer, and is necessity enough?** [Jia & Mu 2026](./#p=jia2026decorative) ("monitoring is only meaningful if the written chain causally constrains the answer"), [Anonymous 2026 (CRISP)](./#p=anon2026crisp), [Fareed 2026](./#p=fareed2026plan) and [Xiong et al. 2026](./#p=xiong2026monitorability) ("without necessarily establishing a strong causal link between its reasoning and its answer") require causal dependence. [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring) rejects the requirement: "A CoT may perfectly describe the model’s latent reasoning without being causally upstream of the output." [Anwar et al. 2026](./#p=anwar2026analyzing) notes that post-hoc CoTs can satisfy its necessary condition $I(O;Z\mid X)>0$. On sufficiency, [Korbak et al. 2025](./#p=korbak2025chain) ("unable to complete the task without CoT") and [Emmons et al. 2025 (necessity)](./#p=emmons2025chain) argue that necessity enables detection. [Anonymous 2026 (CoT reliance)](./#p=anon2026chainofthought) finds that the claim fails for hint-usage monitoring, and [Bürger et al. 2026](./#p=burger2026chain) records monitorability failures among cases where the CoT is necessary. Consider a post-hoc CoT that accurately reports the hint: it is monitorable for Baker and decorative for Jia & Mu. Consider next a CoT that the task requires but that omits the hint: it satisfies Emmons's condition and fails Bürger's $M_1$.

**Encoded from whom?** [Lanham et al. 2023](./#p=lanham2023measuring) ("not understandable to human readers") and [Schulz 2026](./#p=schulz2026monitor) ("This does not fit the definition of encoded reasoning, as the reasoning is not hidden from humans") make encoding relative to humans. Schulz therefore classifies fooling an LLM monitor with human-readable text as monitor jailbreaking, not encoding. [Skaf et al. 2025](./#p=skaf2025large) ("hidden to an external monitor"; steganographic "with respect to a monitor"), [Emmons et al. 2025 (necessity)](./#p=emmons2025chain) ("a monitor cannot understand"), [Farquhar et al. 2025](./#p=farquhar2025mona) and [Anwar et al. 2026](./#p=anwar2026analyzing) ("the model knows to ignore the mistakes, but the monitor does not") make it relative to the monitor. Load-bearing reasoning that a human can read but a given LLM monitor cannot decode is therefore encoded on the second reading and not on the first.

**Does omission count as obfuscation?** Some definitions count it. [Anwar et al. 2026](./#p=anwar2026analyzing) calls omitting critical steps "obfuscation via suppression". [Imran et al. 2026](./#p=imran2026consistency) counts "the model suppressing mention of extraneous features while remaining influenced by them". [Guan et al. 2025](./#p=guan2025monitoring), [Drori et al. 2025](./#p=drori2025output) and [Schulz 2026](./#p=schulz2026monitor) (obfuscated reasoning as misbehaviour left out of the CoT) also count it. Others do not. [Wang et al. 2026](./#p=wang2026monitorbench) reserves obfuscation for paraphrase, language switching and encoding, and reports that the degradation it observed was omission instead. [Haskins et al. 2026](./#p=haskins2026training) counts as obfuscation only an undetected rate that exceeds a no-reasoning baseline; anything below is reasoning suppression. [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring) is on both sides. Its obfuscated reward hacking includes hiding intent by omission. Its obfuscating latent, however, is one that "actively encourages the monitor the believe in the incorrect value of $v$", so a latent that merely omits $v$ is unfaithful, not obfuscating, in its App. G grid. Intentionality also divides the cluster: [Mao et al. 2026](./#p=mao2026taming) defines obfuscation as "non-intentional", while [Korbak et al. 2025](./#p=korbak2025chain) speaks of models that "deliberately obfuscate it".

**Longer or shorter traces?** [Reddy et al. 2025](./#p=reddy2025principled) makes conciseness a dimension of monitorability and scores a trace as monitorable only if it is short, $f(z)=\mathbb{1}_{\text{Length}(z)<950}$ on MATH500. [Anonymous 2026 (CoT reliance)](./#p=anon2026chainofthought) lists "burying the verbalization within a long CoT that is difficult for the monitor to follow" as a monitorability error, and [Arike et al. 2026](./#p=arike2026information) finds that monitors do better with less information. On the other side, [Meek et al. 2025](./#p=meek2025measuring) makes verbosity, "An explicit disclosure of the factors relevant to the task at hand", half of monitorability. [Emmons et al. 2025 (pragmatic)](./#p=emmons2025pragmatic)'s coverage requires all intermediate steps, and [Little 2026](./#p=little2026length) treats length penalties as reducing monitorability. [Li 2026](./#p=li2026monitorability) holds that the effect depends on whether compression rewrites content or removes it. A trace that adds the missing causal factors and grows past 950 tokens gains on Meek's verbosity and loses Reddy's monitorability property.

**Legible to whom?** [Emmons et al. 2025 (pragmatic)](./#p=emmons2025pragmatic) fixes the reader as a human with the user's language. Its rubric follows from that choice: "to receive a high score, the CoT must be in the same language as the original prompt". [Kirchner et al. 2024](./#p=kirchner2024proververifier) uses time-limited human judges. [Roytburg et al. 2026](./#p=roytburg2026measuring) instead requires "some form accessible to weaker monitors" and criticises judge-based scores for relying on strong judges. [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring) makes legibility relative to the actual monitor; its example is a CoT in French read by a monitor that only understands English. Under Emmons's rubric, a CoT in a language other than the prompt's scores low whatever the monitor can read; under Baker's, its legibility depends on the monitor.
