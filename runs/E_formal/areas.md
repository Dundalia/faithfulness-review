# Search areas (one agent each)

## causal_abstraction
Causal abstraction and identifiability: when a high-level causal model is a faithful description of a network's computation, graded or approximate faithfulness, and when concept variables can be identified from a model's activations.
Seeds: Geiger et al. 2025, "Causal abstraction: a theoretical foundation for mechanistic interpretability" (JMLR, arXiv 2301.04709); Geiger et al. 2021, "Causal abstractions of neural networks"; Beckers & Halpern 2019, "Abstracting causal models"; Rubenstein et al. 2017, "Causal consistency of structural equation models"; "Faithfulness through Causal Abstraction" (https://openreview.net/pdf?id=b0rXC856Sm); "Causal Abstraction Inference under Lossy Representations" (arXiv 2509.21607). Dhanya Sridhar's group (list at https://www.dsridhar.com/publications): "Position: Causality is Key for Interpretability Claims to Generalise" (ICML 2025), "Causal Differentiating Concepts: Interpreting LM Behavior via Causal Representation Learning" (NeurIPS 2025), "From Isolation to Entanglement: When Do Interpretability Methods Identify and Disentangle Known Concepts?" (ACL 2026), "Unsupervised Causal Abstractions Discovery" (UAI 2026 workshop), "Who Guards the Guardians? The Challenges of Evaluating Identifiability of Learned Representations" (UAI 2026). For the Sridhar papers, state precisely what each defines or proves and how it bears on defining CoT faithfulness.
Questions: the formal definition of a faithful interpretation or explanation; has anyone applied causal abstraction to CoT or to verbalized reasoning; identifiability conditions for concept variables in LLMs.

## actual_causation
Halpern-Pearl actual causation, explanation and responsibility, and causal explanation in explainable AI.
Seeds: Halpern & Pearl 2005, "Causes and explanations: a structural-model approach", Part I (causes) and Part II (explanations); Halpern 2015, "A modification of the Halpern-Pearl definition of causality"; Halpern 2016, "Actual Causality" (book); Chockler & Halpern 2004, "Responsibility and blame: a structural-model approach"; work by Chockler, Halpern, Kroening and colleagues explaining image classifiers with actual causality; Beckers 2022, "Causal explanations and XAI"; Tian & Pearl 2000 on probabilities of causation; Watson et al. 2021, "Local explanations via necessity and sufficiency"; Galhotra et al. 2021, "Explaining black-box algorithms using probabilistic contrastive counterfactuals"; Miller 2019, "Explanation in artificial intelligence: insights from the social sciences"; "Causality without causal models" (arXiv 2511.21260).
Questions: the formal definition of an explanation and of a correct or faithful one; whether any of it has been applied to LLM rationales, self-explanations or CoT; graded responsibility.

## agency_honesty
Formal definitions of belief, honesty, lying, deception, intent, scheming, sandbagging and hidden objectives.
Seeds: Ward et al. 2023, "Honesty is the best policy: defining and mitigating AI deception" (structural causal games); Halpern & Kleiman-Weiner 2018, "Towards formal definitions of blameworthiness, intention, and moral responsibility"; Everitt et al. 2021, "Agent incentives: a causal perspective"; Hammond et al. 2023, "Reasoning about causality in games"; Evans et al. 2021, "Truthful AI: developing and governing AI that does not lie"; Christiano et al. 2021, the Eliciting Latent Knowledge report; Burns et al. 2023, "Discovering latent knowledge in language models without supervision"; Pacchiardi et al. 2023 on lie detection in black-box LLMs; Hubinger et al. 2019, "Risks from learned optimization" (deceptive alignment); work defining sandbagging and alignment faking.
Questions: formal definitions of belief, intent and deception that could ground the honesty and scheming concepts; whether any connects to CoT or to the faithfulness of stated reasoning.

## information_verification
Information-theoretic and verification-based definitions: monitorability, legibility and checkability, steganography and encoded reasoning.
Seeds: Baker et al. 2025 (in the map: faithfulness of a latent as a sufficient statistic for a property); Anwar et al. 2026 (in the map, key anwar2026analyzing: information-theoretic monitorability); Xu et al. 2020, "A theory of usable information under computational constraints"; Cachin 1998, "An information-theoretic model for steganography"; Hopper, Langford & von Ahn 2002, "Provably secure steganography"; Motwani et al. 2024, "Secret collusion among generative AI agents"; de Witt et al., "Perfectly secure steganography using minimum entropy coupling"; Roger & Greenblatt 2023 (in the map); Anil et al. 2021, "Learning to give checkable answers with prover-verifier games"; Kirchner et al. 2024 (in the map); Irving et al. 2018, "AI safety via debate"; Brown-Cohen et al., "Scalable AI safety via doubly-efficient debate".
Questions: monitor-relative formal definitions of monitorability or oversight; security definitions for encoded reasoning; formal legibility.

## xai_faithfulness_theory
Formal and axiomatic criteria for the faithfulness of explanations in explainable AI and NLP, including impossibility results, and formal criteria for introspection and self-reports.
Seeds: Sundararajan et al. 2017, "Axiomatic attribution for deep networks"; Yeh et al. 2019, "On the (in)fidelity and sensitivity of explanations"; Jacovi & Goldberg 2020 (in the map) and 2021, "Aligning faithful interpretations with their social attribution"; DeYoung et al. 2020, ERASER (in the map); Hase & Bansal 2020 on simulatability; Lyu et al. 2024, "Towards faithful model explanation in NLP: a survey"; Doshi-Velez & Kim 2017; Lipton 2018, "The mythos of model interpretability"; Bilodeau et al. 2024, "Impossibility theorems for feature attribution"; Barez et al. 2025, "Chain-of-thought is not explainability"; Lindsey 2025 on emergent introspective awareness and its criteria; Comsa & Shanahan 2025 on introspection in LLMs.
Questions: axiomatic or formal definitions of a faithful explanation; impossibility results; whether any covers free-text or CoT explanations; formal criteria for introspection.
