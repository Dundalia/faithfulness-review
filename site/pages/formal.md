# Formal Definitions of Faithfulness, Monitorability and Related Concepts

Papers on chain-of-thought (CoT) faithfulness and monitorability define their concepts mostly in words, and most of their mathematical definitions describe a way to measure a concept, not the concept itself. This page asks whether any field defines these concepts mathematically, and what is missing for a definition of CoT faithfulness built on Halpern and Pearl's theory of actual causation, which says when one event caused another in a particular situation. To answer, it compares the definitions in the CoT literature with those in 23 papers from causal inference, game theory, information theory, cryptography and explainable AI. Formal definitions of most of the thirteen concepts exist, but they are spread over several approaches, each covering a subset of the concepts (the 23 papers form six approaches, one with only verbal criteria for introspection), and no approach covers all thirteen. The literature search for this page found no work in any field that defines the faithfulness of a CoT as actual causation, or as a causal abstraction of its written steps (treating the steps as a simple causal model of the network's computation). Definitions of faithfulness, monitorability and steganography in terms of information depend only on statistical relations, not on interventions, so a CoT written after the answer was already computed can satisfy them. Structural causal games, which extend causal models with agents who choose their actions, are the one approach that defines belief, intention and deception together and also contains Halpern-Pearl actual causation.

## What this page compares

This site maps research on CoT faithfulness and monitorability, and "the map" below means its collection of papers. The map's [Definitions page](page.html?doc=definitions) shows that papers on CoT faithfulness and monitorability define their concepts mostly in words. Only 350 of its 3,414 definitions are formal (mathematical), and the labels described below say that 244 of those 350 define only a way to measure or test a concept. Those 3,414 definitions are quoted from papers in the map's original collection of 217 papers. This page calls them *the definitions in the CoT literature*.

This page asks four questions. Does any field give a formal definition of the concepts themselves, and not only of ways to measure them? Which approaches define which concepts? Does one approach cover all of them? And what is missing for a formal causal definition built on Halpern-Pearl actual causality, which the [Actual Causality page](page.html?doc=actual-causality) summarizes? The thirteen concepts are faithfulness; completeness and soundness; post-hoc rationalization; self-report and introspection; honesty, lying and deception; belief and intention; scheming and hidden objectives; monitorability; legibility and checkability; obfuscation; steganography and encoded reasoning; simulatability; and counterfactuals.

Of the definitions in the CoT literature, 350 are formal, and they come from 132 of the 217 papers that the map held when the definitions were collected (all definitions are listed in [one table](https://github.com/Dundalia/faithfulness-review/blob/main/definitions.csv)). According to automatic labels (described in the last section), 106 of them define the notion itself (83 do only that, and 23 define both the notion and a way to measure it) and 244 define only a way to measure or test it.

To look beyond the CoT literature, the literature search for this page verified 113 works, and 23 of them were added to the map, which now holds 240 papers. These 23 papers come mostly from causal inference, game theory, information theory, cryptography and explainable AI, and all of them except the two papers on introspection, which state criteria in words, give mathematical definitions. They were added so that their definitions can be compared with those of the CoT literature, and this page calls them *the 23 formal-theory papers*. From these 23 papers, 291 formal definitions were extracted and checked: 261 define the notion itself (233 do only that, and 28 define both the notion and a way to measure it) and 30 define a way to measure it. All 641 formal definitions (350 from the CoT literature and 291 from the 23 formal-theory papers) can be filtered in the [Formal tab](./#tab=formal), each with a verbatim quote. The 291 definitions from the formal-theory papers also give their assumptions and, for 267 of them, a formula.

The 23 formal-theory papers use six approaches:

- **Structural causal models.** A system is described by variables and equations that say how each variable depends on others; causes, explanations and direct and indirect effects (the parts of an effect that bypass or pass through intermediate variables) are defined by what would happen if some variables were set by intervention.
- **Causal abstraction.** A simple, high-level causal model (for example, an algorithm) faithfully describes a complex, low-level one (for example, a neural network) when intervening on the low-level model and translating the result gives the same outcome as making the matching intervention on the high-level model.
- **Structural causal games.** Structural causal models are extended with agents who choose their actions to get the highest utility, so that belief, intention and deception can be defined from how the agents' choices would change.
- **Information-based definitions.** A property holds when an observer (a monitor, a decoder or a class of predictors, often limited in what it can compute or see) can, or cannot, extract enough information about a target from what it observes, such as the CoT.
- **Explanation theory from explainable AI.** These definitions say when an explanation of a classifier's prediction, stated in terms of the input features, is faithful, sufficient or minimal.
- **Criteria for introspection.** These are conditions, stated in words rather than mathematically, that a model's report about its own internal state must meet to count as introspection.

## Main findings

**1. The CoT literature formalizes measurements, and its formal definitions of the notions themselves rarely use a causal model.** Of the 350 formal definitions in the CoT literature, 244 (70%) define a test, metric or estimator. Only 5 of the 106 that define a notion itself use a causal model. Two define potential outcomes and the monitor's observation map ([Anonymous 2026 (causal bounds)](./#p=anon2026improving)), one a counterfactual reasoning step ([Khanzadeh 2026](./#p=khanzadeh2026project)), one a direct-effect intervention on a concept ([Matton et al. 2025](./#p=matton2025walk)), and one faithfulness itself. That one, [Wang et al. 2026a](./#p=wang2026case), calls a CoT-based decision process faithful "if the instruction influences the final answer only through the generated CoT". It states the condition as a conditional independence, which says that once the CoT is known, the instruction tells nothing more about the answer: $p_{\theta}(y\mid z,x)=p_{\theta}(y\mid x)$, with $z$ the instruction, $x$ the CoT and $y$ the answer.

**2. The 23 formal-theory papers fall into six approaches; five give formal definitions of the notions themselves, the sixth gives verbal criteria for introspection, and each approach covers a subset of the concepts.** The 23 formal-theory papers yield 61 definitions from structural causal models, 92 from causal abstraction, 37 from structural causal games, 42 information-based definitions, 43 from explanation theory in explainable AI and 16 from criteria for introspection. The final coverage review of the literature search for this page concludes that "No single formal framework covers all the concepts in scope", and the table below agrees. Nine of the 13 concepts have a formal definition of the notion itself in some approach. Post-hoc rationalization and scheming have none. Obfuscation has formal definitions only by analogy: opacity in discrete event systems (an outside observer can never be sure that the system is, was or will be in a secret state), goal obfuscation in planning, and a CoT that no polynomial-time monitor can read. Introspection has only verbal criteria.

**3. The literature search for this page found no work in any field that defines CoT faithfulness as actual causation or as a causal abstraction of the written steps. The 217 papers of the map's original collection never mention actual causation.** The 23 formal-theory papers define actual causes and explanations motivated by the predictions of machine-learning models ([Beckers 2022](./#p=beckers2022causal)) and for any model in which counterfactuals are defined ([Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality)). They also define actual causes in games, where an agent's decision can be the cause ([Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty)). They never define them for the steps of a CoT. The closest work to verbalized reasoning that the causal-abstraction part of the literature search for this page found is CAuSE ([Bandyopadhyay et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06814)). Its natural-language explainer of a multimodal classifier, "trained via interchange intervention, forms a causal abstraction of the underlying classifier". (An interchange intervention runs a model on one input while setting some of its internal variables to the values they take on another input.) [Yu et al. 2025](./#p=yu2025causal) define probabilities of necessity and sufficiency for CoT steps relative to the correct answer $y$: the probability that the CoT leads to the correct answer with the step, and fails to when the step is replaced. So they measure whether a step is needed for the correct answer, not whether the model's own answer, right or wrong, depended on it. None of the 217 papers of the original collection uses the terms actual cause, actual causation or actual causality, and none cites Halpern, Beckers or Chockler. Of these papers, 7 cite causal-abstraction papers of Geiger and colleagues (5 of them a paper with causal abstraction in its title), 7 cite [Pearl 2001](./#p=pearl2001direct) (2 of them through its 2022 reprint) and 1 cites [Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty).

**4. Structural causal games are the one approach that defines belief, intention and deception together and contains Halpern-Pearl actual causation.** [Hammond et al. 2023](./#p=hammond2023reasoning) "generalise Pearl’s causal hierarchy of models to the game-theoretic domain", and "An SCG can be seen as an SCM without parameters for the decision variables." Pearl's causal hierarchy is the ladder of association, intervention and counterfactuals. SCG stands for structural causal game and SCM for structural causal model: in a game, the agents' decisions are not fixed by equations but chosen by the agents. [Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty) define belief (Definition 3.1), intention (Definition 3.4) and deception (Definition 3.7) in these games. They adapt the modified Halpern-Pearl definition of actual causality to them (Definition 6.6), and prove that "If an agent i intentionally causes X = x then Di is an actual cause" of $X=x$ (Theorem 3.6), where $D^i$ denotes the decision variables of agent $i$. [Ward et al. 2024](./#p=ward2024reasons) add "a purely behavioural notion of intent" and use it to assess the intentions of reinforcement-learning agents and language models. Lying and honesty stay outside this approach: Ward et al. write that "In contrast to lying, deception does not require a linguistic statement".

**5. Causal abstraction defines the faithfulness of an interpretation of a network's computation, both exactly and in degrees. Without a restriction on the alignment map (the map from the network's states to the states of the interpretation), the definition is vacuous.** [Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal) write that "Approximate transformation (Section 2.4) provides the needed flexible notion of graded faithfulness". [Beckers et al. 2019](./#p=beckers2019approximate) measure the abstraction error by a worst-case distance $d_\tau$ and require it to be at most $\alpha$. [Sutter et al. 2025](./#p=sutter2025non) prove that "under reasonable assumptions, any neural network can be mapped to any algorithm, rendering this unrestricted notion of causal abstraction trivial and uninformative". Their alignment maps reach 100% interchange-intervention accuracy (the share of interchange interventions on which the network and the algorithm give the same output) on the indirect object identification task with randomly initialized language models. [Xia & Bareinboim 2025](./#p=xia2025causal) show that most existing definitions "are not well-defined when considering lossy abstraction functions", that is, functions that map low-level values with different downstream effects to the same high-level value. A map from a network's states to a few written steps is likely to be lossy in their sense.

**6. The information-theoretic definitions of faithfulness, monitorability and steganography are observational, so a CoT written after the answer was computed can satisfy them.** Observational means that they depend only on the joint probability distribution of the CoT, the answer and other variables, not on what would happen under an intervention. [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring) call a latent faithful with respect to a property $v$ if it "is a sufficient statistic for $v$", that is, if it contains all the information relevant to predicting $v$. [Jia et al. 2026](./#p=jia2026faithfulness) define sufficiency as a low $H(A\mid C)$: little uncertainty about the answer $A$ remains once the CoT $C$ is known. They define completeness as $I(P;A\mid C)\approx 0$: once the CoT is known, the prompt $P$ adds almost no information about the answer. [Anwar et al. 2026 (information theory)](./#p=anwar2026analyzing) bound the accuracy uplift of the Bayes-optimal CoT monitor over the Bayes-optimal prompt-only monitor through the conditional mutual information, $0\le\delta\le\sqrt{2I(Y;Z\mid X)}$. The bound says that the uplift $\delta$ is at most the square root of twice the information that the CoT $Z$ adds about the monitored attribute $Y$ beyond the prompt $X$. They also define a monitor-class version, $I_{\mathcal{V}}(Y;Z\mid X)$, which counts only the information that monitors in a class $\mathcal V$ can use (usable information). A CoT that ends by stating an answer the model had already computed, which the final answer then copies, meets Jia et al.'s sufficiency and completeness, meets Wang et al.'s conditional independence, and meets Baker et al.'s condition whenever the monitored property is a function of the answer. It also makes any attribute of the answer as predictable from the CoT as from the answer itself. [Mohammadkhani et al. 2026](./#p=mohammadkhani2026hidden) separate leakage from readability. Beyond a size-dependent threshold, solving a covert task (a task on an input that the monitor cannot see) "necessarily leaks a near-linear amount of information about the covert task input into the CoT", yet "this leakage need not be readable" by polynomial-time monitors. [Anwar et al. 2026 (steganography)](./#p=anwar2026decision) define steganography as a gap in usable information between a Receiver, who knows the encoding, and a trusted Sentinel, who does not. This definition needs no cover distribution (a reference distribution of innocent texts), but it does not ask whether the model's computation used the hidden content.

## The six approaches in detail

Each subsection says what the approach defines, what it assumes, which of the thirteen concepts it covers and what it cannot express.

### 1. Structural causal models: actual causes, explanations, responsibility and mediation

Five papers supply 61 definitions in this approach: [Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality) (11), [Beckers 2022](./#p=beckers2022causal) (16), [Beckers 2025](./#p=beckers2025large) (13), [Yu et al. 2025](./#p=yu2025causal) (9) and [Pearl 2001](./#p=pearl2001direct) (12).

**What it defines.** An actual cause is what made an outcome happen in one particular situation. Under the modified Halpern-Pearl definition, $\vec X=\vec x$ is an actual cause of $\varphi$ in a causal setting $(M,\vec u)$ when three conditions hold ([Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality), Definition 2.1; [Actual Causality, Section 2.2](page.html?doc=actual-causality#22-a-formal-definition-of-actual-cause)). A causal setting is a causal model $M$ together with a context $\vec u$, which fixes the variables determined outside the model. In words: the cause and the outcome both occur (AC1); some other value of the cause, with some other variables $\vec W$ held at their actual values, would make the outcome false (AC2); and no part of the cause already does this (AC3):

$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{AC1}:&\ (M,\vec u)\models(\vec X=\vec x)\land\varphi.\\\mathrm{AC2}:&\ \exists\vec W\subseteq\mathcal V\setminus\vec X,\vec w^*,\vec x' :\ (M,\vec u)\models\vec W=\vec w^*\ \land\ (M,\vec u)\models[\vec X\leftarrow\vec x',\vec W\leftarrow\vec w^*]\neg\varphi.\\\mathrm{AC3}:&\ \nexists\vec X'\subsetneq\vec X\text{ whose actual assignment satisfies AC2}.\end{aligned}$$

An explanation is a minimal sufficient cause relative to an epistemic state $K$, the set of contexts an agent considers possible ([Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality), Definition 6.1; [Actual Causality, Section 7.1](page.html?doc=actual-causality#71-explanation-the-basic-definition)). In words: wherever in $K$ the explanation and the outcome $\psi$ hold, part of the explanation, possibly with other facts, is an actual cause of $\psi$ (EX1(a)); everywhere in $K$, setting the explanation by intervention makes $\psi$ hold (EX1(b)); no smaller part of the explanation does both (EX2); and the explanation and $\psi$ hold together somewhere in $K$ (EX3):

$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{EX1(a)}:&\ \forall\vec u\in K,\ (M,\vec u)\models\vec X=\vec x\land\psi\Rightarrow\exists(X=x)\in(\vec X=\vec x),\vec Y=\vec y:\ X=x\land\vec Y=\vec y\text{ is a cause of }\psi.\\\mathrm{EX1(b)}:&\ \forall\vec u'\in K,\ (M,\vec u')\models[\vec X\leftarrow\vec x]\psi.\\\mathrm{EX2}:&\ \nexists\vec X'\subsetneq\vec X\text{ satisfying EX1(a), EX1(b) with the restricted assignment}.\\\mathrm{EX3}:&\ \exists\vec u\in K:\ (M,\vec u)\models\vec X=\vec x\land\psi.\end{aligned}$$

The explanation is nontrivial if some context in $K$ has the outcome without the explanation (EX4). Under the original Halpern-Pearl definition, a cause has degree of responsibility $1/(k+1)$, where $k$ is the size of a minimal witness set: the smallest set $\vec W$ of other variables that must be set to suitable values (which, under the original definition and unlike in the modified AC2 above, need not be their actual values) for the outcome to depend counterfactually on the cause. The degree of blame is the expected responsibility over an epistemic state ([Actual Causality, Sections 6.1 and 6.2](page.html?doc=actual-causality#61-a-naive-definition-of-responsibility); [Chockler & Halpern 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0312038)). [Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality) generalize the definitions of actual cause and explanation from structural equations to any class of models in which counterfactuals are defined. Causes and explanations can then be formulas "involving disjunctions, negations, beliefs, and nested counterfactuals (none of which can be handled by the Halpern-Pearl definition)". [Beckers 2022](./#p=beckers2022causal), motivated by explanations of machine-learning predictions, defines sufficient explanations, counterfactual explanations and actual causes. A sufficient explanation names an intervention together with the network of variables that must stay undisturbed for the intervention to guarantee the output. [Beckers 2025](./#p=beckers2025large) represents an autoregressive LLM as a nondeterministic causal model, one whose mechanisms are random rather than fixed equations, with the prompt, every generated token and the output as its variables. It proves that "Given a nondeterministic causal model M that corresponds to an LLM, M satisfies the simple semantics", so a counterfactual on a changed prompt is distributed like a new run on that prompt. [Yu et al. 2025](./#p=yu2025causal) carry Pearl's probabilities of causation over to CoT steps. The probability of necessity and sufficiency of a step $s_t$ is the probability that the CoT $S$ gives the correct answer $y$ while the altered CoT $S'$ does not, $\operatorname{PNS}(S,s_t,q):=P(A_S=y,A_{S'}\ne y)$, where $S'$ replaces step $s_t$ and regenerates the steps after it.

Mediation splits the effect of a variable $X$ on an outcome $Y$ into a direct part and a part that passes through intermediate variables, the mediators $Z$. [Pearl 2001](./#p=pearl2001direct) defines natural direct and indirect effects through nested counterfactuals, and path-specific effects (effects along a chosen set of edges) through an edge-subgraph of the causal graph (Definition 8). At the unit level $u$, $X$ has a natural direct effect on $Y$ (Definition 4) and an indirect effect through the mediators $Z$ (Definition 6) when, respectively, the first and the second inequality below hold. The first says that changing $X$ from $x^*$ to $x$ changes $Y$ even when $Z$ is held at the value it takes under $x^*$. The second says that $Y$ changes when $X$ stays at $x^*$ but $Z$ is set to the value it takes under $x$:

$$Y_{x^*}(u)\ne Y_{x,Z_{x^*}(u)}(u),\qquad Y_{x^*}(u)\ne Y_{x^*,Z_x(u)}(u).$$

**What it assumes.** A causal model whose endogenous variables (those determined inside the model), ranges and equations are given, and an actual context. Halpern & Pass 2025 need only a model "where counterfactuals are defined", and Beckers 2025 replaces deterministic equations with stochastic mechanisms. The verdicts depend on the model: the choice of variables and their ranges can change whether something is a cause ([Actual Causality, Chapter 4](page.html?doc=actual-causality#chapter-4--the-art-of-causal-modeling)).

**Concepts it covers.** Actual causation, explanation, responsibility and blame; completeness and soundness, as the sufficiency and minimality of an explanation; counterfactuals; with an epistemic state, explanation relative to a reader; with mediation, and as readings proposed on this page, faithfulness as complete mediation by the CoT and post-hoc rationalization as a null indirect effect.

**What it cannot express.** The definitions take the variables as given and say nothing about which variables a written step refers to. Beckers 2025 treats the generated tokens as variables, but its counterfactuals change only the prompt. Intention needs an epistemic state over causal models ([Halpern & Kleiman-Weiner 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05903)), belief and deception need the extension to games (approach 3), and a monitor's computational limits need the information-based approach (approach 4).

### 2. Causal abstraction: when a simple causal model describes a network

Five papers supply 92 definitions: [Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal) (31), [Beckers & Halpern 2018](./#p=beckers2019abstracting) (10), [Beckers et al. 2019](./#p=beckers2019approximate) (14), [Xia & Bareinboim 2025](./#p=xia2025causal) (22) and [Sutter et al. 2025](./#p=sutter2025non) (15).

**What it defines.** A high-level model $M^*$ is an exact transformation of a low-level model $M$ under a state map $\tau$ and an intervention map $\omega$ when intervening and then translating gives the same states as translating the intervention and solving the high-level model ([Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal), Definition 25). Here $\tau$, the alignment map, translates low-level states into high-level states; $\omega$ translates low-level interventions into high-level ones; and $\operatorname{Solve}(M_I)$ is the state that model $M$ reaches under intervention $I$. The formula states that, for every intervention that $\omega$ translates, the translated low-level result equals the high-level result:

$$\forall I\in\operatorname{Domain}(\omega),\qquad\tau\bigl(\operatorname{Solve}(M_I)\bigr)=\operatorname{Solve}\bigl(M^*_{\omega(I)}\bigr).$$

[Beckers & Halpern 2018](./#p=beckers2019abstracting) order the definitions from Rubenstein et al.'s exact transformation through uniform transformation and $\tau$-abstraction to strong abstraction. They define constructive abstraction as the case in which each high-level variable is computed from its own group of low-level variables. Graded faithfulness is approximate transformation ([Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal), Definition 41). The formula scores how similar the translated low-level result is to the high-level result for each intervention, and summarizes these scores over interventions drawn from a distribution $P$:

$$\mathcal S_{I\sim P}\!\left[\operatorname{Sim}\!\left(\tau(\operatorname{Solve}(M_I)),\operatorname{Solve}(M^*_{\omega(I)})\right)\right],$$

Here the similarity function, the distribution $P$ over interventions and the summary statistic $\mathcal S$ are free choices; interchange intervention accuracy, LIME fidelity and causal scrubbing are special cases. [Beckers et al. 2019](./#p=beckers2019approximate) call $M_H$ a $\tau$-$\alpha$ approximate abstraction of $M_L$ when the worst-case distance $d_\tau(M_L,M_H)$ is at most $\alpha$. In [Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal) (Remark 53), a hidden vector completely mediates the effect of the inputs on the outputs exactly when the abstraction it induces has the chain structure input, hidden vector, output, with no direct edge from input to output. [Xia & Bareinboim 2025](./#p=xia2025causal) relax the abstract invariance condition, which states "that two low-level values cannot map to the same high-level value if they have different downstream effects". They define a projected abstraction as a partial projection of a $\tau$-abstraction (a smaller model in which each variable is only partly represented) that moves the lost information into latent variables.

**What it assumes.** Intervention access to the low-level model, here the network's internals; a hypothesized high-level model; the maps $\tau$ and $\omega$ and the class they are drawn from; for constructive abstraction, a partition of the low-level variables.

**Concepts it covers.** Faithfulness of an interpretation, exact and graded; counterfactuals as interchange interventions; mediation; through a class of alignment maps, the linear representation hypothesis (that concepts are encoded as directions in activation space).

**What it cannot express.** A CoT is text, not a causal model, and no paper found by the literature search for this page reads the steps of a CoT as a high-level model. Without a restriction on $\tau$ the definition is vacuous ([Sutter et al. 2025](./#p=sutter2025non)), and explanations need not be unique: [Méloux et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20914) report "systematic non-identifiability", with several circuits, interpretations, algorithms and subspaces explaining the same behavior. Abstraction says nothing about agents, beliefs or observers.

### 3. Structural causal games: belief, intention and deception of agents

Three papers supply 37 definitions: [Hammond et al. 2023](./#p=hammond2023reasoning) (16), [Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty) (11) and [Ward et al. 2024](./#p=ward2024reasons) (10).

**What it defines.** [Hammond et al. 2023](./#p=hammond2023reasoning) define structural causal games (Definition 22) and counterfactual queries in games (Definition 23). Their rationality relations, which say which policies the agents choose, let interventions propagate into the agents' choice of policies (a policy is an agent's rule for choosing a decision from what it observes). They also adapt Halpern and Kleiman-Weiner's degree of blameworthiness (Definition 34). An agent $i$ that responds to a proposition $\phi$ believes it if it acts as though it observed $\phi$ to be true ([Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty), Definition 3.1). In the formula, the first bracket says that the agent responds to $\phi$: its decision would differ if it observed $\phi$ false rather than true. The second says that its actual decision is the one it makes when it observes $\phi$ true:

$$\operatorname{Bel}_i(\phi;\pi,\mathbf e)\iff \bigl[D^i_{\phi=\bot}(\pi_{i(\phi)},\mathbf e)\ne D^i_{\phi=\top}(\pi_{i(\phi)},\mathbf e)\bigr]\land\bigl[D^i(\pi,\mathbf e)=D^i_{\phi=\top}(\pi_{i(\phi)},\mathbf e)\bigr].$$

An agent $S$ deceives an agent $T$ about $\phi$ if $S$ intentionally causes $T$'s decision, $T$ believes $\phi$, $\phi$ is false and $S$ does not believe $\phi$ (Definition 3.7):

$$\operatorname{Dec}_{S,T}(\phi;\pi,\mathbf e)\iff \operatorname{IntCause}_S\!\left(D^T=D^T(\pi,\mathbf e);\pi^S,\mathbf e\right)\land \operatorname{Bel}_T(\phi;\pi,\mathbf e)\land\neg\phi(\pi,\mathbf e)\land\neg\operatorname{Bel}_S(\phi;\pi,\mathbf e).$$

Intention (Definition 3.4) holds when guaranteeing the outcome under an alternative policy would make that policy just as good for the agent. [Ward et al. 2024](./#p=ward2024reasons) define subjective intention, with the model read as the agent's subjective beliefs (Definition 6), and behavioral intention, in which the agent's policy changes when the intended outcomes are fixed (Definition 13). They show the two equivalent "under the assumption that the agent is robustly optimal".

**What it assumes.** A known game with utilities and a common prior (the agents share the same prior beliefs); Theorem 3.6 "follows from the assumption that the agents share the correct causal model"; belief is behavioral and is defined only for propositions the agent responds to; intention needs reference alternative policies.

**Concepts it covers.** Belief, intention and deception; actual causation inside games; counterfactuals with adaptive agents; blame; components of scheming such as instrumental control incentives.

**What it cannot express.** Lying and honesty of statements, since deception needs no statement; the content of a CoT; belief when the agent does not respond to the proposition; scheming and hidden objectives as such.

### 4. Information-based definitions: what an observer can extract

Five papers supply 42 definitions: [Anwar et al. 2026 (steganography)](./#p=anwar2026decision) (18), [Xu et al. 2020](./#p=xu2020theory) (4), [Mohammadkhani et al. 2026](./#p=mohammadkhani2026hidden) (11), [Yin 2026](./#p=yin2026opacity) (7) and [Cachin 1998](./#p=cachin1998information) (2). Checkability defined through prover-verifier games comes from papers in the CoT literature. In a prover-verifier game, one model (the prover) writes solutions and another (the verifier) checks them.

**What it defines.** Predictive $\mathcal V$-information, or usable information, measures how much side information $X$ improves the best prediction of $Y$ available to a constrained class $\mathcal V$ of predictors ([Xu et al. 2020](./#p=xu2020theory), Definitions 2 and 3). In the formula, $H_{\mathcal V}(Y\mid X)$ is the lowest expected log loss for $Y$ that a predictor in $\mathcal V$ can reach when it sees $X$. The second quantity, $I_{\mathcal V}(X\to Y)$, is how much that loss falls when the predictor sees $X$ rather than nothing:

$$H_{\mathcal V}(Y\mid X)=\inf_{f\in\mathcal V}\mathbb E_{x,y\sim X,Y}\big[-\log f[x](y)\big],\qquad I_{\mathcal V}(X\to Y)=H_{\mathcal V}(Y\mid\varnothing)-H_{\mathcal V}(Y\mid X).$$

As Xu et al. put it, "Unlike Shannon’s mutual information and in violation of the data processing inequality, V-information can be created through computation." [Anwar et al. 2026 (information theory)](./#p=anwar2026analyzing) define its conditional form for a hypothesis class of monitors, $I_{\mathcal V}(Y;Z\mid X)$, and use it in a sufficient condition for a practical CoT monitor to outperform a practical output monitor (Definition E.3 and Proposition E.5). [Anwar et al. 2026 (steganography)](./#p=anwar2026decision) generalize it to arbitrary utilities and define the steganographic gap between a Receiver who knows the encoding and a trusted Sentinel who does not (Equations 7 and 8). The first quantity below is how much seeing the signal $Z$, on top of the other inputs $X^{\mathrm{aud}}$, raises the best utility that the Receiver's predictors can reach for $Y$. The gap is this gain for the Receiver minus the same gain for the Sentinel:

$$I_{\mathrm{rec}}(Z\to Y)=U^{u}_{\mathcal V_{\mathrm{rec}}}(Y\mid X^{\mathrm{aud}},Z)-U^{u}_{\mathcal V_{\mathrm{rec}}}(Y\mid X^{\mathrm{aud}}),\qquad\Delta_{\mathrm{steg}}(Z)=I_{\mathrm{rec}}(Z\to Y)-I_{\mathrm{sen}}(Z\to Y).$$

A signal is steganographic if the gap is positive. The classical definition of [Cachin 1998](./#p=cachin1998information) is $\varepsilon$-security, $D(P_C\|P_S)\le\varepsilon$: the relative entropy between the distribution of innocent texts (covertext) and the distribution of texts carrying a hidden message (stegotext) is at most $\varepsilon$. It is recovered as binary detection with an omnipotent Sentinel, where the normalized gap equals $1-\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$, one minus the total variation distance, and is at least $1-\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}$ for an $\varepsilon$-secure system (Proposition D.1). [Mohammadkhani et al. 2026](./#p=mohammadkhani2026hidden) call a CoT unreadable when no polynomial-size monitor predicts any predicate of the covert input better than the best constant guess, up to a negligible term (Theorem B.14). A negligible term shrinks faster than any inverse polynomial in $N$. The formula states this for every sequence $\mathcal A$ of polynomial-size monitors, one for each $N$, every predicate $P_N$ of the covert input $X$ and every distribution $\mathcal D_N$ of that input (independent of the private random bits $R$):

$$\forall\,\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{A}_N\}_N\ \text{poly-size}:\quad \Pr_{X\sim\mathcal{D}_N,\ R\sim\mathcal{U}_{r(N)}}\big[\mathcal{A}_N(\mathrm{CoT}_N(X;R))=P_N(X)\big]\le\max_{b\in\{0,1\}}\Pr_{X\sim\mathcal{D}_N}\big[b=P_N(X)\big]+\mathrm{negl}(N).$$

They prove that, under plausible cryptographic assumptions, a one-layer Transformer with private random bits can meet this criterion while the mutual information between the covert input and the CoT stays near-linear in the input length. [Yin 2026](./#p=yin2026opacity) surveys opacity in discrete event systems, defined by "The intruder can never know for sure that the system is, was, or will be in a secret state". That is, every secret behavior has an observationally identical non-secret one, where $P$ maps a behavior to what the intruder observes:

$$\forall s\in L_{\mathrm{sec}},\ \exists t\in L_{\text{non-sec}}:\ P(s)=P(t).$$

Yin contrasts infinite-step opacity with diagnosability, which "can be viewed as an “always eventually know” requirement, while infinite-step opacity corresponds to an “always not know” requirement". Yin does not mention monitorability; reading diagnosability as monitorability is this page's analogy. In the CoT literature, checkability is the completeness and soundness of a verifier. In [Kirchner et al. 2024](./#p=kirchner2024proververifier), the prover "always generates correct and convincing solutions" and no "sneaky" prover from the same class of models can make the verifier accept an incorrect one. [Kim & Lee 2026](./#p=kim2026mitigating) require only that the verifier accept the prover's correct answers, but also that "there exists no adversarial proof that can trick the verifier into accepting an incorrect answer".

**What it assumes.** A predictor, decoder or monitor class; a target attribute or task with a utility; for the steganographic gap, a Sentinel of matched public capability (Assumption E.3), since with a weaker Sentinel "a positive gap can arise even without any steganographic encoding" (Proposition E.10); for Cachin, a known covertext distribution; for opacity, an intruder who knows the system model; for unreadability, a cryptographic assumption and private randomness; for checkability, a verifier and ground-truth correctness labels, and in Kirchner et al. 2024 a class of prover models.

**Concepts it covers.** Monitorability, legibility and checkability, steganography, obfuscation through opacity and unreadability, and simulatability as predictor accuracy.

**What it cannot express.** Whether the model's computation used the information. The quantities are properties of the joint distribution of the CoT and a target, so a CoT computed after the answer can carry the same information as one the answer was computed from. The steganographic gap is blind to payloads that carry no consequential information about the audited task, and the information-theoretic footprint of Mohammadkhani et al. (how much information about the covert input the CoT contains) measures leakage, not readability.

### 5. Explanation theory from explainable AI: faithful explanations of a classifier

Three papers supply 43 definitions: [Dasgupta et al. 2022](./#p=dasgupta2022framework) (13), [Marques-Silva 2022](./#p=marquessilva2022logic) (22) and [Bilodeau et al. 2022](./#p=bilodeau2022impossibility) (8).

**What it defines.** [Dasgupta et al. 2022](./#p=dasgupta2022framework) show that the faithfulness of an explanation system "can be captured by two properties, consistency and sufficiency". With $f$ the classifier, $e$ the explainer, $\mu$ the deployment distribution and $A(x',\pi)$ the relation stating that explanation $\pi$ applies to $x'$, local consistency and local sufficiency are defined as follows (Definitions 1 and 3). Local consistency $m_c(x)$ is the probability that an input with the same explanation as $x$ gets the same label as $x$. Local sufficiency $m_s(x)$ is the probability that an input to which the explanation of $x$ applies gets the same label as $x$:

$$m_c(x):=\Pr_{x'\sim\mu|C_\pi}\bigl(f(x')=f(x)\bigr),\quad C_\pi:=\{x:e(x)=\pi\};\qquad m_s(x):=\Pr_{x'\sim\mu|C_x}\bigl(f(x')=f(x)\bigr),\quad C_x:=\{x':A(x',e(x))\}.$$

Global consistency equals the accuracy of a Gibbs decoder that predicts the label from the explanation. A Gibbs decoder is randomized: given an explanation, it returns each label with the probability that the model gives that label to inputs with this explanation. [Marques-Silva 2022](./#p=marquessilva2022logic) defines abductive explanations (AXp): a weak AXp is a set of features whose values guarantee the prediction for every completion of the other features, and an AXp is a subset-minimal one (Equations 4 and 5):

$$\operatorname{WeakAXp}(X):=\forall\mathbf x\in\mathbb F,\ \left[\bigwedge_{i\in X}(x_i=v_i)\right]\to(\kappa(\mathbf x)=c),\qquad\operatorname{AXp}(X):=\operatorname{WeakAXp}(X)\land\forall X'\subsetneq X,\neg\operatorname{WeakAXp}(X').$$

Contrastive explanations (CXp) are their minimal hitting-set duals. A contrastive explanation is a subset-minimal set of features whose change, with the other features kept at their values, can change the prediction, and the duality means that each CXp is a minimal set of features that shares at least one feature with every AXp, and the reverse. [Bilodeau et al. 2022](./#p=bilodeau2022impossibility) prove that, "for moderately rich model classes (easily satisfied by neural networks)", any feature attribution method that is complete and linear, such as Integrated Gradients or SHAP, "can provably fail to improve on random guessing for inferring model behaviour". Here completeness means that the attributions sum to the model's output at $x$ minus its average output, $\sum_{j}\Phi(f,\mu,x)_j=f(x)-\mathbb E_{X\sim\mu}f(X)$.

**What it assumes.** A known classifier over input features; an explanation that is a property of the input; a deployment distribution; for computing an AXp, a faithful logical encoding of the classifier and a sound and complete consistency oracle (a solver that always correctly decides whether a set of logical constraints can be satisfied).

**Concepts it covers.** Faithfulness of input-output explanations; completeness and soundness of an explanation as sufficiency and minimality, distinct from the completeness axiom of feature attributions; counterfactuals as contrastive explanations; simulatability as decoder accuracy; legibility as intelligibility.

**What it cannot express.** An explanation of intermediate computation: a CoT names intermediate steps, not input features, and these definitions test only whether the explanation predicts the input-output map. Both decoders in Dasgupta et al., the Gibbs decoder among them, use the exact distribution $\Pr(y\mid\pi)$ of the model's labels given the explanation, so consistency does not model a bounded human or monitor.

### 6. Criteria for introspection: conditions stated in words

Two papers supply 16 definitions: [Lindsey 2025](./#p=lindsey2025emergent) (12) and [Song et al. 2025 (privileged self-access)](./#p=song2025privileged) (4).

**What it defines.** A model demonstrates introspective awareness if it "can describe some aspect of its internal state" while meeting four criteria ([Lindsey 2025](./#p=lindsey2025emergent), Section 3): accuracy; grounding, "The model’s description of its internal state must causally depend on the aspect that is being described"; internality, "it should not route through the model’s sampled outputs"; and metacognitive representation. [Song et al. 2025 (privileged self-access)](./#p=song2025privileged) propose that introspection is "any process which yields information about internal states of the AI through a process that is more reliable than any process with equal or lower computational cost available to a third party without special knowledge of the situation". The definitions extracted for this page record no formula for any of these criteria. The only formula they record for this approach is a measurement, Lindsey's net performance: the rate of correct identifications on injection trials (in which a concept is injected into the model's activations) minus the rate of false positives on control trials:

$$\mathrm{TPR}-\mathrm{FPR}.$$

Lindsey himself glosses grounding counterfactually ("if the internal state were different, the description would change accordingly"). The notes recorded with the extracted definitions read internality as a path-specific effect that excludes paths through sampled outputs, and observe that Song et al.'s criterion resembles usable information under computational constraints.

**What it assumes.** Access to the true internal state, which Lindsey fixes by injecting concept vectors into the activations; for Song et al., a class of third-party processes and a measure of computational cost.

**Concepts it covers.** Self-report accuracy and introspection; simulatability, through the comparison with third-party prediction.

**What it cannot express.** As stated, none of the criteria is a mathematical condition. Lindsey writes of metacognitive representations that "Demonstrating metacognitive representations is difficult to do directly, and we do not do so in this work." The criteria concern descriptions of "some aspect of its internal state", which Lindsey's accuracy criterion extends to "the internal mechanisms they use to perform calculations". But his experiments elicit reports of injected concepts and of whether an output was intended, not accounts of a reasoning process.

## What is missing from the existing definitions

- **No definition of CoT faithfulness as actual causation or as an abstraction of the written steps was found.** Actual causes and explanations are defined over the variables of a given model, and causal abstraction relates a network to an algorithm; no paper treats the steps of a CoT as formulas whose truth and causal role are evaluated in such a model. CAuSE ties natural-language explanations to causal abstraction only for classifiers, and Yu et al.'s step-level probabilities of causation are defined relative to the correct answer.
- **No account of which causal variables a written step refers to.** Halpern-Pearl verdicts depend on the model, and Halpern & Pass allow causes that are formulas, but both presuppose the variables. Results from Dhanya Sridhar's group bear on how such variables could be fixed. Causal differentiating concepts are "interpretable latent directions in LM activations that must be changed to elicit a different model behavior", recovered as disentangled representations "under a particular assumption about the sparsity" ([Goyal et al. 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/5faf0f518f39e4d6b2b595e3ca2fe0c4-Abstract-Conference.html)). Sparse shift autoencoders "are identifiable from paired observations which differ in multiple unknown concepts, but not all", that is, from such data they recover the true concepts up to their order and scale ([Joshi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12179)). [Saulus et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19594) show "that observations generated by a low-rank graph induce latents that form a causal abstraction" and give identifiability results for those latents. [Joshi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16698) add that counterfactual claims about what the model would have output "remain largely unverifiable without controlled supervision". A faithfulness claim about a single prompt, such as that the answer would have differed without a given step, is a counterfactual claim of this kind.
- **Unrestricted abstraction is vacuous.** Any definition of faithfulness through abstraction must fix the class of alignment maps: [Sutter et al. 2025](./#p=sutter2025non) conclude that if the linearity constraint on alignment maps is lifted, "we are left with no principled way to balance the inherent trade-off between these maps’ complexity and accuracy". A map from activations to written steps is likely to merge states with different downstream effects, which would violate the abstract invariance condition and call for the projected abstractions of [Xia & Bareinboim 2025](./#p=xia2025causal).
- **Information-based definitions that a CoT written after the answer also satisfies.** The sufficient-statistic definition of [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring), the sufficiency and completeness of [Jia et al. 2026](./#p=jia2026faithfulness), the conditional independence of [Wang et al. 2026a](./#p=wang2026case) and the usable-information definitions of monitorability and steganography are all properties of a joint distribution. A CoT that restates a precomputed answer can meet all of them, so they cannot separate faithful reasoning from rationalization without an interventional condition, that is, a condition on what happens under an intervention.
- **Concepts with only measurements, verbal criteria or components.** Post-hoc rationalization has no definition in any of the six approaches. The CoT literature describes it in words, for instance as reasoning "produced after a certain conclusion has already been guaranteed" ([Lanham et al. 2023](./#p=lanham2023measuring)). Its formal definitions of the notion itself reach post-hoc rationalization only indirectly: as the negation of a faithfulness definition ([Wang et al. 2026a](./#p=wang2026case)), as the failure of a necessity condition that "distinguishes genuine reasoning from post-hoc rationalization" ([Jia et al. 2026](./#p=jia2026faithfulness)), or as performative steps after the answer is already extractable ([Parekh & Goyal 2026](./#p=parekh2026drop)). Scheming and hidden objectives have components and settings but no definition of the concept; the nearest is a behavioral definition of defeat devices that covers evaluation-gated scheming ([Ferrara 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28863)). Introspection has verbal criteria. Obfuscation of a CoT is formal only by analogy, with opacity, unreadability and goal obfuscation in planning ([Kulkarni et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06137)). No definition of encoded reasoning requires that the model's computation causally use the hidden content. The closest, the non-transparent CoT of [Kaufmann et al. 2026](./#p=kaufmann2026aligned), is use-based but not causal: it compares what a human reads in the CoT with "what information the model’s downstream computation extracts from the CoT text". Honesty and lying have no definition in a causal approach. Lying is defined formally only in works on logic that are not in the map. Honesty is defined by a logical schema ([Evans et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06674)) and by a fixed-point condition on performative predictions, which are predictions that affect what they predict ([Oesterheld et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17601)). In the CoT literature, it is defined behaviorally, as accuracy under pressure ([Wang et al. 2025 (lie detection)](./#p=wang2025evaluating)).

## Toward a single formal definition: five conjectures

The evidence points to a combination of approaches rather than to any single one. The five proposals below are conjectures, not results; each names the papers it rests on.

**Faithfulness as actual causation in a restricted abstraction.** One could read each step $s_i$ of a CoT $c$ as a formula $\delta(s_i)$ over the variables of a high-level model $H$ that approximately abstracts the network $L$ under an alignment map from a restricted class $\mathcal T$. One would then require the steps to be sound and complete with respect to the actual causes of the answer $A=a$. A possible form is below. It says three things: some alignment map in $\mathcal T$ makes $H$ an approximate abstraction of $L$ with error at most $\alpha$; the formula of every step is an actual cause of the answer in $H$ (soundness); and every formula of interest that is an actual cause of the answer follows from the steps (completeness):

$$\mathrm{Faith}_\alpha(c)\iff\exists\tau\in\mathcal T:\ d_\tau(L,H)\le\alpha\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Cause}_{(H,\vec u)}\big(\delta(s_i),A=a\big)\ \land\ \forall\psi\in\Psi:\ \big[\mathrm{Cause}_{(H,\vec u)}(\psi,A=a)\Rightarrow\textstyle\bigwedge_i\delta(s_i)\models\psi\big],$$

where $\Psi$ is a set of formulas of interest. This rests on the approximate abstraction of [Beckers et al. 2019](./#p=beckers2019approximate), the projected abstractions of [Xia & Bareinboim 2025](./#p=xia2025causal) for a lossy $\delta$, the restriction of $\mathcal T$ demanded by [Sutter et al. 2025](./#p=sutter2025non), the formula-level causes of [Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality) (Definition 4.1), the complete and sound credit sets of [Luo et al. 2026](./#p=luo2026removal), and the identifiability results of [Goyal et al. 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/5faf0f518f39e4d6b2b595e3ca2fe0c4-Abstract-Conference.html) and [Joshi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12179) for fixing the variables of $H$.

**Faithfulness and plausibility as explanation relative to two epistemic states.** A CoT could count as plausible to a reader when its content is an explanation of the answer, in the sense of EX1 to EX3, relative to the reader's model and epistemic state $K_R$. It could count as faithful when it is also an explanation in the abstraction of the model's own computation. Post-hoc rationalization would then be a CoT that is plausible but contains no actual cause in the model's abstraction. This is the reading proposed in the actual-causation part of the literature search for this page: "an explanation that is sufficient in the reader's model but not an actual cause in the model's". Nontriviality (EX4) would require the CoT to tell the reader something the answer alone does not. This rests on Definitions 6.1 and 6.3 of [Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality) and on the general definition of explanation with model fragments, that is, partial causal models ([Actual Causality, Section 7.3](page.html?doc=actual-causality#73-the-general-definition-of-explanation)).

**The monitor as an agent in a structural causal game.** The model and the monitor could be two agents: the model chooses the CoT and the action, and the monitor chooses whether to flag. Monitorability would be the usable information that a class of monitors has about a causally defined target, for instance whether a hidden-objective variable is an actual cause of the action under the game version of actual causality. Obfuscation would be the model intentionally causing the monitor's decision while the monitor ends ignorant of the target. Deception of the monitor would be Definition 3.7 applied to it, and steganography would model the Receiver and the Sentinel as further agents. The final coverage review of the literature search for this page preferred this approach as the one to unify the others ("they are compatible with Halpern-Pearl and can include the monitor as an agent"). It rests on [Hammond et al. 2023](./#p=hammond2023reasoning), [Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty) (belief, intention, deception, ignorance and actual causality in games), [Ward et al. 2024](./#p=ward2024reasons) (behavioral intention without utilities), [Xu et al. 2020](./#p=xu2020theory), [Anwar et al. 2026 (information theory)](./#p=anwar2026analyzing), [Anwar et al. 2026 (steganography)](./#p=anwar2026decision) and the polynomial-time monitor class of [Mohammadkhani et al. 2026](./#p=mohammadkhani2026hidden).

**Mediation for faithfulness, post-hoc rationalization and internality.** The conditional independence of [Wang et al. 2026a](./#p=wang2026case) has an interventional counterpart: a null natural direct effect of the instruction on the answer, with the CoT as mediator. Post-hoc rationalization could be a null natural indirect effect through the CoT together with a CoT that predicts the answer, and Lindsey's internality a path-specific effect that excludes the edges through sampled tokens. This rests on [Pearl 2001](./#p=pearl2001direct) (Definitions 4, 6 and 8), on Remark 53 of [Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal), on [Lindsey 2025](./#p=lindsey2025emergent), and on [Beckers 2025](./#p=beckers2025large) for a semantics of counterfactuals over sampled tokens, which that paper gives only for interventions on the prompt.

**Introspection as resource-bounded privileged access.** Song et al.'s criterion could be stated as an inequality between the reliability of the self-report about an internal state and the best reliability achievable by a class of third-party predictors of equal or lower cost. This would be a usable-information comparison in the sense of [Xu et al. 2020](./#p=xu2020theory), with Lindsey's grounding added as counterfactual dependence of the report on the state. This rests on [Song et al. 2025 (privileged self-access)](./#p=song2025privileged), [Lindsey 2025](./#p=lindsey2025emergent) and [Xu et al. 2020](./#p=xu2020theory).

## How this page was made

1. **Finding works that the CoT literature cites often.** A script, `runs/E_formal/cited_formal.py`, scanned the reference lists of the 217 papers of the original collection for works cited by at least three papers and absent from the map. It found 225 arXiv identifiers and 234 title segments that match no title in the map exactly, 9 of which are in fact papers in the map cited with an appended arXiv number or a typo (`runs/E_formal/cited_outside.json`). Among them are works the literature search later examined, such as Lindsey 2025 (cited by 5 papers), Anil et al. 2021 (3) and Evans et al. 2021 (3), and the title segment "chain of thought is not explainability" (11).
2. **Labeling the formal definitions of the CoT literature.** Codex, an AI coding agent, labeled each of the 350 formal definitions in `definitions.csv` with whether it defines the notion itself, a way to measure it, or both, and with the formalism it uses (`runs/E_formal/formal_labels.json`).
3. **Literature search.** Five search agents, one per area (actual causation, causal abstraction, agency and honesty, information and verification, faithfulness theory in explainable AI), opened and verified 101 candidate works. A final coverage review built the concept-by-concept coverage table, searched for approaches the five areas had missed and added 13 works (`runs/E_formal/critic.json`). After one duplicate was merged, 113 works remain in `runs/E_formal/candidates.json`, 30 of them rated essential. The 23 formal-theory papers are the essential works that were not yet in the map, except Halpern & Pearl 2005 (Parts I and II), Halpern 2016 and Chockler & Halpern 2004, whose definitions the [Actual Causality page](page.html?doc=actual-causality) already covers (chapters 2, 6 and 7 of Halpern 2016).
4. **Extracting and checking the definitions.** GPT-6.1 Sol, run through Codex, read the papers of three of the five batches, and Claude read those of the other two after Codex reached its weekly usage cap. Together they wrote the papers' entries in the map and extracted 334 formal definitions with verbatim quotes, formulas, assumptions and locations. Claude checked each batch against the full texts and PDFs. It applied 39 fixes, dropped 47 definitions that were background, informal or out of scope, added 4 and corrected 32 passages of the papers' entries. A final pass fixed 7 more definitions (two formulas that KaTeX, the math renderer, could not render, the two Cachin definitions and three Yin definitions), recorded in `runs/E_formal/checks_render.json`. Of the resulting 291 definitions in `formal.csv`, all 291 quotes match the cached full texts verbatim. The 2 definitions from Cachin 1998 were first written from the abstract and from the restatement in Anwar et al. 2026 (steganography); they were checked against the author's revised full text once it was cached.
5. **Counts.** Every count on this page was recomputed with Python from `formal.csv`, `definitions.csv`, `runs/E_formal/formal_labels.json`, the files of the literature search and the cached full texts.

6. **Checking the claims.** Every factual claim on this page (134 claims) was checked twice against the papers and the data, each time by three Claude agents; the second time, the agents did not see the first verdicts. The first check replaced 31 claims (22 overstated, 5 understated, 4 wrong) with the strongest statement the evidence supports; the second replaced 8 more (2 overstated, 4 understated, 2 wrong). Codex did not check this page, because it had reached its weekly cap, so the two checks are independent runs of the same model rather than of two models. The claims and both verdicts are in `runs/E_formal/claims_formal.json`.
