# Formal Definitions of Faithfulness, Monitorability and Related Concepts

The map's [Definitions page](page.html?doc=definitions) shows that papers on chain-of-thought (CoT) faithfulness and monitorability define their concepts mostly in words: only 350 of its 3,414 definitions are formal, and the labels below put 244 of those 350 at the level of a measurement. This page asks four questions: does a formal definition of the concepts themselves exist in any field, which frameworks define which concepts, does one framework cover all of them, and what is missing for a formal causal definition built on Halpern-Pearl actual causality, summarized on the [Actual Causality page](page.html?doc=actual-causality). In brief, formal definitions of most of the concepts exist, but they are spread over several framework families that each cover a subset (the 23 added papers form six families, one of which gives only verbal criteria for introspection); no framework covers all thirteen concepts; and none of the five searches or the completeness check found a work in any field that defines the faithfulness of a CoT as actual causation or as a causal abstraction of its written steps.

The definitions catalogue ([`definitions.csv`](https://github.com/Dundalia/faithfulness-review/blob/main/definitions.csv)) holds 350 rows of kind formal, from 132 of the 217 papers that the map held when the catalogue was built. Codex labeled 106 of them as defining a concept (83 the concept alone, 23 both the concept and its measurement) and 244 as defining only a measurement. To look beyond the catalogue, five search agents and a completeness check verified 113 candidate works, and 23 formal-framework papers among them were added to the map, which now holds 240 papers. From these 23 papers, 291 formal definitions were extracted and checked: 261 define a concept (233 the concept alone, 28 both) and 30 define a measurement. All 641 formal rows can be filtered in the [Formal tab](./#tab=formal), each with a verbatim quote; the 291 framework rows also give assumptions and, for 267 of them, a formula.

## Main findings

**1. The catalogue formalizes measurements, and its formal definitions of concepts rarely use a causal model.** Of the 350 formal rows, 244 (70%) define a test, metric or estimator. Only 5 of the 106 concept-level rows use a causal model: two define potential outcomes and the monitor's observation map ([Anonymous 2026 (causal bounds)](./#p=anon2026improving)), one a counterfactual reasoning step ([Khanzadeh 2026](./#p=khanzadeh2026project)), one a direct-effect intervention on a concept ([Matton et al. 2025](./#p=matton2025walk)), and one faithfulness itself. That one, [Wang et al. 2026a](./#p=wang2026case), calls a CoT-based decision process faithful "if the instruction influences the final answer only through the generated CoT" and states the condition as the conditional independence $p_{\theta}(y\mid z,x)=p_{\theta}(y\mid x)$, with $z$ the instruction, $x$ the CoT and $y$ the answer.

**2. The 23 framework papers fall into six families; five give formal definitions of concepts themselves, the sixth gives verbal criteria for introspection, and each family covers a subset of the concepts.** The 23 framework papers yield 61 definitions from structural causal models, 92 from causal abstraction, 37 from structural causal games, 42 from observer-relative information, 43 from explanation theory in explainable AI and 16 from criteria for introspection. The completeness check concludes that "No single formal framework covers all the concepts in scope", and the table below agrees. Nine of the 13 concepts have a formal definition of the concept itself in some framework; post-hoc rationalization and scheming have none, obfuscation has formal definitions only by analogy (opacity in discrete event systems, goal obfuscation in planning, a CoT that no polynomial-time monitor can read), and introspection has only verbal criteria.

**3. None of the searches found a work in any field that defines CoT faithfulness as actual causation or as a causal abstraction of the written steps, and the 217 papers in the map before the formal sweep never mention actual causation.** The framework papers define actual causes and explanations motivated by the predictions of machine-learning models ([Beckers 2022](./#p=beckers2022causal)) and for any model in which counterfactuals are defined ([Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality)), and actual causes in games, where an agent's decision can be the cause ([Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty)), never for the steps of a CoT. The closest work to verbalized reasoning that the causal-abstraction search found is CAuSE ([Bandyopadhyay et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06814)), whose natural-language explainer of a multimodal classifier, "trained via interchange intervention, forms a causal abstraction of the underlying classifier". [Yu et al. 2025](./#p=yu2025causal) define probabilities of necessity and sufficiency for CoT steps relative to the correct answer $y$, so they measure whether a step is needed for the correct answer, not whether the model's own answer, right or wrong, depended on it. None of the 217 papers in the map before the formal sweep uses the terms actual cause, actual causation or actual causality, and none cites Halpern, Beckers or Chockler; 7 cite causal-abstraction papers of Geiger and colleagues (5 of them a paper with causal abstraction in its title), 7 cite [Pearl 2001](./#p=pearl2001direct) (2 of them through its 2022 reprint) and 1 cites [Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty).

**4. Structural causal games are the one framework that defines belief, intention and deception together and contains Halpern-Pearl actual causation.** [Hammond et al. 2023](./#p=hammond2023reasoning) "generalise Pearl’s causal hierarchy of models to the game-theoretic domain", and "An SCG can be seen as an SCM without parameters for the decision variables." [Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty) define belief (Definition 3.1), intention (Definition 3.4) and deception (Definition 3.7) in these games, adapt the modified Halpern-Pearl definition of actual causality to them (Definition 6.6), and prove that "If an agent i intentionally causes X = x then Di is an actual cause" of $X=x$ (Theorem 3.6). [Ward et al. 2024](./#p=ward2024reasons) add "a purely behavioural notion of intent" and use it to assess the intentions of reinforcement-learning agents and language models. Lying and honesty stay outside the framework: Ward et al. write that "In contrast to lying, deception does not require a linguistic statement".

**5. Causal abstraction defines the faithfulness of an interpretation of a network's computation, exactly and in degrees, and without a restriction on the alignment map the definition is vacuous.** [Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal) write that "Approximate transformation (Section 2.4) provides the needed flexible notion of graded faithfulness", and [Beckers et al. 2019](./#p=beckers2019approximate) measure the abstraction error by a worst-case distance $d_\tau$ and require it to be at most $\alpha$. [Sutter et al. 2025](./#p=sutter2025non) prove that "under reasonable assumptions, any neural network can be mapped to any algorithm, rendering this unrestricted notion of causal abstraction trivial and uninformative", and their alignment maps reach 100% interchange-intervention accuracy on the indirect object identification task with randomly initialized language models. [Xia & Bareinboim 2025](./#p=xia2025causal) show that most existing definitions "are not well-defined when considering lossy abstraction functions", and a map from a network's states to a few written steps is likely to be lossy in their sense.

**6. The information-theoretic definitions of faithfulness, monitorability and steganography are observational, so a CoT written after the answer was computed can satisfy them.** [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring) call a latent faithful with respect to a property $v$ if it "is a sufficient statistic for $v$"; [Jia et al. 2026](./#p=jia2026faithfulness) define sufficiency as a low $H(A\mid C)$ and completeness as $I(P;A\mid C)\approx 0$; [Anwar et al. 2026 (information theory)](./#p=anwar2026analyzing) bound the accuracy uplift of the Bayes-optimal CoT monitor over the Bayes-optimal prompt-only monitor through the conditional mutual information, $0\le\delta\le\sqrt{2I(Y;Z\mid X)}$, and define a monitor-class version, $I_{\mathcal{V}}(Y;Z\mid X)$. A CoT that ends by stating an answer the model had already computed, which the final answer then copies, meets Jia et al.'s sufficiency and completeness, meets Wang et al.'s conditional independence, and meets Baker et al.'s condition whenever the monitored property is a function of the answer; it also makes any attribute of the answer as predictable from the CoT as from the answer itself. [Mohammadkhani et al. 2026](./#p=mohammadkhani2026hidden) separate leakage from readability: beyond a size-dependent threshold, solving a covert task "necessarily leaks a near-linear amount of information about the covert task input into the CoT", yet "this leakage need not be readable" by polynomial-time monitors. [Anwar et al. 2026 (steganography)](./#p=anwar2026decision) define steganography as a gap in usable information between a Receiver and a Sentinel, which needs no cover distribution but does not ask whether the model's computation used the hidden content.

## Frameworks

### Structural causal models: actual causation, explanation, responsibility, mediation

Five papers supply 61 definitions in this family: [Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality) (11), [Beckers 2022](./#p=beckers2022causal) (16), [Beckers 2025](./#p=beckers2025large) (13), [Yu et al. 2025](./#p=yu2025causal) (9) and [Pearl 2001](./#p=pearl2001direct) (12).

**What it defines.** Under the modified Halpern-Pearl definition, $\vec X=\vec x$ is an actual cause of $\varphi$ in a causal setting $(M,\vec u)$ when three conditions hold ([Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality), Definition 2.1; [Actual Causality, Section 2.2](page.html?doc=actual-causality#22-a-formal-definition-of-actual-cause)):

$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{AC1}:&\ (M,\vec u)\models(\vec X=\vec x)\land\varphi.\\\mathrm{AC2}:&\ \exists\vec W\subseteq\mathcal V\setminus\vec X,\vec w^*,\vec x' :\ (M,\vec u)\models\vec W=\vec w^*\ \land\ (M,\vec u)\models[\vec X\leftarrow\vec x',\vec W\leftarrow\vec w^*]\neg\varphi.\\\mathrm{AC3}:&\ \nexists\vec X'\subsetneq\vec X\text{ whose actual assignment satisfies AC2}.\end{aligned}$$

An explanation is a minimal sufficient cause relative to an epistemic state $K$, the set of contexts an agent considers possible ([Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality), Definition 6.1; [Actual Causality, Section 7.1](page.html?doc=actual-causality#71-explanation-the-basic-definition)):

$$\begin{aligned}\mathrm{EX1(a)}:&\ \forall\vec u\in K,\ (M,\vec u)\models\vec X=\vec x\land\psi\Rightarrow\exists(X=x)\in(\vec X=\vec x),\vec Y=\vec y:\ X=x\land\vec Y=\vec y\text{ is a cause of }\psi.\\\mathrm{EX1(b)}:&\ \forall\vec u'\in K,\ (M,\vec u')\models[\vec X\leftarrow\vec x]\psi.\\\mathrm{EX2}:&\ \nexists\vec X'\subsetneq\vec X\text{ satisfying EX1(a), EX1(b) with the restricted assignment}.\\\mathrm{EX3}:&\ \exists\vec u\in K:\ (M,\vec u)\models\vec X=\vec x\land\psi.\end{aligned}$$

The explanation is nontrivial if some context in $K$ has the outcome without the explanation (EX4). Under the original definition, the degree of responsibility of a cause is $1/(k+1)$ for a minimal witness set of size $k$, and the degree of blame is expected responsibility over an epistemic state ([Actual Causality, Sections 6.1 and 6.2](page.html?doc=actual-causality#61-a-naive-definition-of-responsibility); [Chockler & Halpern 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0312038)). [Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality) abstract the definitions of actual cause and explanation from structural equations to any family of models in which counterfactuals are defined, so that causes and explanations can be formulas "involving disjunctions, negations, beliefs, and nested counterfactuals (none of which can be handled by the Halpern-Pearl definition)". [Beckers 2022](./#p=beckers2022causal), motivated by explanations of machine-learning predictions, defines sufficient explanations, counterfactual explanations and actual causes; a sufficient explanation names an intervention together with the network that must stay undisturbed for the intervention to guarantee the output. [Beckers 2025](./#p=beckers2025large) represents an autoregressive LLM as a nondeterministic causal model whose variables are the prompt, every generated token and the output, and proves that "Given a nondeterministic causal model M that corresponds to an LLM, M satisfies the simple semantics", so a counterfactual on a changed prompt is distributed like a new run on that prompt. [Yu et al. 2025](./#p=yu2025causal) carry Pearl's probabilities of causation over to CoT steps: $\operatorname{PNS}(S,s_t,q):=P(A_S=y,A_{S'}\ne y)$, where $S'$ replaces step $s_t$ and regenerates the steps after it, and $y$ is the correct answer.

[Pearl 2001](./#p=pearl2001direct) defines natural direct and indirect effects through nested counterfactuals, and path-specific effects through an edge-subgraph of the causal graph (Definition 8). At the unit level $u$, $X$ has a natural direct effect on $Y$ (Definition 4) and an indirect effect through the mediators $Z$ (Definition 6) when, respectively,

$$Y_{x^*}(u)\ne Y_{x,Z_{x^*}(u)}(u),\qquad Y_{x^*}(u)\ne Y_{x^*,Z_x(u)}(u).$$

**Assumptions.** A causal model whose endogenous variables, ranges and equations are given, and an actual context; Halpern & Pass 2025 need only a model "where counterfactuals are defined", and Beckers 2025 replaces deterministic equations with stochastic mechanisms. The verdicts are model-relative: the choice of variables and their ranges can change whether something is a cause ([Actual Causality, Chapter 4](page.html?doc=actual-causality#chapter-4--the-art-of-causal-modeling)).

**Concepts covered.** Actual causation, explanation, responsibility and blame; completeness and soundness as the sufficiency and minimality of an explanation; counterfactuals; with an epistemic state, explanation relative to a reader; with mediation, and as proposed readings, faithfulness as complete mediation by the CoT and post-hoc rationalization as a null indirect effect.

**What it cannot express.** The definitions take the variables as given and say nothing about which variables a written step refers to. Beckers 2025 treats the generated tokens as variables, but its counterfactuals change only the prompt. Intention needs an epistemic state over causal models ([Halpern & Kleiman-Weiner 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05903)), belief and deception need the game extension below, and a monitor's computational limits need the information-theoretic family.

### Causal abstraction

Five papers supply 92 definitions: [Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal) (31), [Beckers & Halpern 2018](./#p=beckers2019abstracting) (10), [Beckers et al. 2019](./#p=beckers2019approximate) (14), [Xia & Bareinboim 2025](./#p=xia2025causal) (22) and [Sutter et al. 2025](./#p=sutter2025non) (15).

**What it defines.** A high-level model $M^*$ is an exact transformation of a low-level model $M$ under a state map $\tau$ and an intervention map $\omega$ when intervening and then translating gives the same states as translating the intervention and solving the high-level model ([Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal), Definition 25):

$$\forall I\in\operatorname{Domain}(\omega),\qquad\tau\bigl(\operatorname{Solve}(M_I)\bigr)=\operatorname{Solve}\bigl(M^*_{\omega(I)}\bigr).$$

[Beckers & Halpern 2018](./#p=beckers2019abstracting) order the definitions from Rubenstein et al.'s exact transformation through uniform transformation and $\tau$-abstraction to strong abstraction, and define constructive abstraction as the case in which each high-level variable is computed from its own cluster of low-level variables. Graded faithfulness is approximate transformation ([Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal), Definition 41):

$$\mathcal S_{I\sim P}\!\left[\operatorname{Sim}\!\left(\tau(\operatorname{Solve}(M_I)),\operatorname{Solve}(M^*_{\omega(I)})\right)\right],$$

where the similarity function, the distribution $P$ over interventions and the summary statistic $\mathcal S$ are free; interchange intervention accuracy, LIME fidelity and causal scrubbing are special cases. [Beckers et al. 2019](./#p=beckers2019approximate) call $M_H$ a $\tau$-$\alpha$ approximate abstraction of $M_L$ when the worst-case distance $d_\tau(M_L,M_H)$ is at most $\alpha$. In [Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal) (Remark 53), a hidden vector completely mediates the effect of the inputs on the outputs exactly when the abstraction it induces has the chain structure input, hidden vector, output, with no direct edge from input to output. [Xia & Bareinboim 2025](./#p=xia2025causal) relax the abstract invariance condition, which states "that two low-level values cannot map to the same high-level value if they have different downstream effects", and define a projected abstraction as a partial projection of a $\tau$-abstraction that moves the lost information into latent variables.

**Assumptions.** Intervention access to the low-level model, here the network's internals; a candidate high-level model; the maps $\tau$ and $\omega$ and the class they are drawn from; for constructive abstraction, a partition of the low-level variables.

**Concepts covered.** Faithfulness of an interpretation, exact and graded; counterfactuals as interchange interventions; mediation; through an alignment class, the linear representation hypothesis.

**What it cannot express.** A CoT is text, not a causal model, and no paper that the searches found reads the steps of a CoT as a high-level model. Without a restriction on $\tau$ the definition is vacuous ([Sutter et al. 2025](./#p=sutter2025non)), and explanations need not be unique: [Méloux et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20914) report "systematic non-identifiability", with several circuits, interpretations, algorithms and subspaces explaining the same behavior. Abstraction says nothing about agents, beliefs or observers.

### Structural causal games

Three papers supply 37 definitions: [Hammond et al. 2023](./#p=hammond2023reasoning) (16), [Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty) (11) and [Ward et al. 2024](./#p=ward2024reasons) (10).

**What it defines.** [Hammond et al. 2023](./#p=hammond2023reasoning) define structural causal games (Definition 22) and counterfactual queries in games (Definition 23), with rationality relations that let interventions propagate into the agents' choice of policies, and adapt Halpern and Kleiman-Weiner's degree of blameworthiness (Definition 34). An agent $i$ that responds to a proposition $\phi$ believes it if it acts as though it observed $\phi$ to be true ([Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty), Definition 3.1):

$$\operatorname{Bel}_i(\phi;\pi,\mathbf e)\iff \bigl[D^i_{\phi=\bot}(\pi_{i(\phi)},\mathbf e)\ne D^i_{\phi=\top}(\pi_{i(\phi)},\mathbf e)\bigr]\land\bigl[D^i(\pi,\mathbf e)=D^i_{\phi=\top}(\pi_{i(\phi)},\mathbf e)\bigr].$$

An agent $S$ deceives an agent $T$ about $\phi$ if $S$ intentionally causes $T$'s decision, $T$ believes $\phi$, $\phi$ is false and $S$ does not believe $\phi$ (Definition 3.7):

$$\operatorname{Dec}_{S,T}(\phi;\pi,\mathbf e)\iff \operatorname{IntCause}_S\!\left(D^T=D^T(\pi,\mathbf e);\pi^S,\mathbf e\right)\land \operatorname{Bel}_T(\phi;\pi,\mathbf e)\land\neg\phi(\pi,\mathbf e)\land\neg\operatorname{Bel}_S(\phi;\pi,\mathbf e).$$

Intention (Definition 3.4) holds when guaranteeing the outcome under an alternative policy would make that policy just as good for the agent. [Ward et al. 2024](./#p=ward2024reasons) define subjective intention, with the model read as the agent's subjective beliefs (Definition 6), and behavioral intention, in which the agent's policy changes when the intended outcomes are fixed (Definition 13), and show the two equivalent "under the assumption that the agent is robustly optimal".

**Assumptions.** A known game with utilities and a common prior; Theorem 3.6 "follows from the assumption that the agents share the correct causal model"; belief is behavioral and is defined only for propositions the agent responds to; intention needs reference alternative policies.

**Concepts covered.** Belief, intention and deception; actual causation inside games; counterfactuals with adaptive agents; blame; components of scheming such as instrumental control incentives.

**What it cannot express.** Lying and honesty of statements, since deception needs no statement; the content of a CoT; belief when the agent does not respond to the proposition; scheming and hidden objectives as such.

### Observer-relative information

Five papers supply 42 definitions: [Anwar et al. 2026 (steganography)](./#p=anwar2026decision) (18), [Xu et al. 2020](./#p=xu2020theory) (4), [Mohammadkhani et al. 2026](./#p=mohammadkhani2026hidden) (11), [Yin 2026](./#p=yin2026opacity) (7) and [Cachin 1998](./#p=cachin1998information) (2). Prover-verifier checkability comes from map papers in the catalogue.

**What it defines.** Predictive $\mathcal V$-information measures how much side information $X$ improves the best prediction of $Y$ available to a constrained family $\mathcal V$ ([Xu et al. 2020](./#p=xu2020theory), Definitions 2 and 3):

$$H_{\mathcal V}(Y\mid X)=\inf_{f\in\mathcal V}\mathbb E_{x,y\sim X,Y}\big[-\log f[x](y)\big],\qquad I_{\mathcal V}(X\to Y)=H_{\mathcal V}(Y\mid\varnothing)-H_{\mathcal V}(Y\mid X).$$

"Unlike Shannon’s mutual information and in violation of the data processing inequality, V-information can be created through computation." [Anwar et al. 2026 (information theory)](./#p=anwar2026analyzing) define its conditional form for a hypothesis class of monitors, $I_{\mathcal V}(Y;Z\mid X)$, and use it in a sufficient condition for a practical CoT monitor to outperform a practical output monitor (Definition E.3 and Proposition E.5). [Anwar et al. 2026 (steganography)](./#p=anwar2026decision) generalize it to arbitrary utilities and define the steganographic gap between a Receiver who knows the encoding and a trusted Sentinel who does not (Equations 7 and 8):

$$I_{\mathrm{rec}}(Z\to Y)=U^{u}_{\mathcal V_{\mathrm{rec}}}(Y\mid X^{\mathrm{aud}},Z)-U^{u}_{\mathcal V_{\mathrm{rec}}}(Y\mid X^{\mathrm{aud}}),\qquad\Delta_{\mathrm{steg}}(Z)=I_{\mathrm{rec}}(Z\to Y)-I_{\mathrm{sen}}(Z\to Y).$$

A signal is steganographic if the gap is positive. The classical definition of [Cachin 1998](./#p=cachin1998information), $\varepsilon$-security $D(P_C\|P_S)\le\varepsilon$ between the covertext and stegotext distributions, is recovered as binary detection with an omnipotent Sentinel, where the normalised gap equals $1-\mathrm{TV}(P_0,P_1)$ and is at least $1-\sqrt{\varepsilon/2}$ for an $\varepsilon$-secure system (Proposition D.1). [Mohammadkhani et al. 2026](./#p=mohammadkhani2026hidden) call a CoT unreadable when no polynomial-size monitor predicts any predicate of the covert input better than the best constant guess, up to a negligible term (Theorem B.14):

$$\forall\,\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{A}_N\}_N\ \text{poly-size}:\quad \Pr_{X\sim\mathcal{D}_N,\ R\sim\mathcal{U}_{r(N)}}\big[\mathcal{A}_N(\mathrm{CoT}_N(X;R))=P_N(X)\big]\le\max_{b\in\{0,1\}}\Pr_{X\sim\mathcal{D}_N}\big[b=P_N(X)\big]+\mathrm{negl}(N).$$

They prove that, under plausible cryptographic assumptions, a one-layer Transformer with private random bits can meet this criterion while the mutual information between the covert input and the CoT stays near-linear in the input length. [Yin 2026](./#p=yin2026opacity) surveys opacity in discrete event systems, defined by "The intruder can never know for sure that the system is, was, or will be in a secret state", that is, every secret behavior has an observationally identical non-secret one:

$$\forall s\in L_{\mathrm{sec}},\ \exists t\in L_{\text{non-sec}}:\ P(s)=P(t).$$

Yin contrasts infinite-step opacity with diagnosability, which "can be viewed as an “always eventually know” requirement, while infinite-step opacity corresponds to an “always not know” requirement". Yin does not mention monitorability; reading diagnosability as monitorability is the map's analogy. In the catalogue, checkability is the completeness and soundness of a verifier: in [Kirchner et al. 2024](./#p=kirchner2024proververifier) the prover "always generates correct and convincing solutions" and no "sneaky" prover from the same model family can make the verifier accept an incorrect one, while [Kim & Lee 2026](./#p=kim2026mitigating) require only that the verifier accept the prover's correct answers but that "there exists no adversarial proof that can trick the verifier into accepting an incorrect answer".

**Assumptions.** A predictor, decoder or monitor class; a target attribute or task with a utility; for the steganographic gap, a Sentinel of matched public capability (Assumption E.3), since with a weaker Sentinel "a positive gap can arise even without any steganographic encoding" (Proposition E.10); for Cachin, a known covertext distribution; for opacity, an intruder who knows the system model; for unreadability, a cryptographic assumption and private randomness; for checkability, a verifier and ground-truth correctness labels, and in Kirchner et al. 2024 a model family of provers.

**Concepts covered.** Monitorability, legibility and checkability, steganography, obfuscation through opacity and unreadability, and simulatability as predictor accuracy.

**What it cannot express.** Whether the model's computation used the information. The quantities are properties of the joint distribution of the CoT and a target, so a CoT computed after the answer can carry the same information as one the answer was computed from. The steganographic gap is blind to payloads that carry no consequential information about the audited task, and the footprint of Mohammadkhani et al. is leakage, not readability.

### Explanation theory in explainable AI

Three papers supply 43 definitions: [Dasgupta et al. 2022](./#p=dasgupta2022framework) (13), [Marques-Silva 2022](./#p=marquessilva2022logic) (22) and [Bilodeau et al. 2022](./#p=bilodeau2022impossibility) (8).

**What it defines.** [Dasgupta et al. 2022](./#p=dasgupta2022framework) show that the faithfulness of an explanation system "can be captured by two properties, consistency and sufficiency". With $f$ the classifier, $e$ the explainer, $\mu$ the deployment distribution and $A(x',\pi)$ the relation stating that explanation $\pi$ applies to $x'$, local consistency and local sufficiency are (Definitions 1 and 3):

$$m_c(x):=\Pr_{x'\sim\mu|C_\pi}\bigl(f(x')=f(x)\bigr),\quad C_\pi:=\{x:e(x)=\pi\};\qquad m_s(x):=\Pr_{x'\sim\mu|C_x}\bigl(f(x')=f(x)\bigr),\quad C_x:=\{x':A(x',e(x))\}.$$

Global consistency equals the accuracy of a Gibbs decoder that predicts the label from the explanation. [Marques-Silva 2022](./#p=marquessilva2022logic) defines abductive explanations: a weak AXp is a set of features whose values guarantee the prediction for every completion of the other features, and an AXp is a subset-minimal one (Equations 4 and 5):

$$\operatorname{WeakAXp}(X):=\forall\mathbf x\in\mathbb F,\ \left[\bigwedge_{i\in X}(x_i=v_i)\right]\to(\kappa(\mathbf x)=c),\qquad\operatorname{AXp}(X):=\operatorname{WeakAXp}(X)\land\forall X'\subsetneq X,\neg\operatorname{WeakAXp}(X').$$

Contrastive explanations (CXp) are their minimal hitting-set duals. [Bilodeau et al. 2022](./#p=bilodeau2022impossibility) prove that, "for moderately rich model classes (easily satisfied by neural networks)", any feature attribution method that is complete and linear, such as Integrated Gradients or SHAP, "can provably fail to improve on random guessing for inferring model behaviour", where completeness means $\sum_{j}\Phi(f,\mu,x)_j=f(x)-\mathbb E_{X\sim\mu}f(X)$.

**Assumptions.** A known classifier over input features; an explanation that is a property of the input; a deployment distribution; for computing an AXp, a faithful logical encoding of the classifier and a sound and complete consistency oracle.

**Concepts covered.** Faithfulness of input-output explanations; completeness and soundness of an explanation as sufficiency and minimality, distinct from the completeness axiom of feature attributions; counterfactuals as contrastive explanations; simulatability as decoder accuracy; legibility as intelligibility.

**What it cannot express.** An explanation of intermediate computation: a CoT names intermediate steps, not input features, and these definitions test only whether the explanation predicts the input-output map. Both decoders in Dasgupta et al. use the exact distribution $\Pr(y\mid\pi)$ of the model's labels given the explanation, so consistency does not model a bounded human or monitor.

### Criteria for introspection

Two papers supply 16 definitions: [Lindsey 2025](./#p=lindsey2025emergent) (12) and [Song et al. 2025 (privileged self-access)](./#p=song2025privileged) (4).

**What it defines.** A model demonstrates introspective awareness if it "can describe some aspect of its internal state" while meeting four criteria ([Lindsey 2025](./#p=lindsey2025emergent), Section 3): accuracy; grounding, "The model’s description of its internal state must causally depend on the aspect that is being described"; internality, "it should not route through the model’s sampled outputs"; and metacognitive representation. [Song et al. 2025 (privileged self-access)](./#p=song2025privileged) propose that introspection is "any process which yields information about internal states of the AI through a process that is more reliable than any process with equal or lower computational cost available to a third party without special knowledge of the situation". formal.csv records no formula for any of these criteria; the only formula it records for this family is a measurement, Lindsey's net performance, the rate of correct identifications on injection trials minus the rate of false positives on control trials:

$$\mathrm{TPR}-\mathrm{FPR}.$$

Lindsey himself glosses grounding counterfactually ("if the internal state were different, the description would change accordingly"); the extraction reads internality as a path-specific effect that excludes paths through sampled outputs, and notes that Song et al.'s criterion resembles usable information under computational constraints.

**Assumptions.** Access to the true internal state, which Lindsey fixes by injecting concept vectors into the activations; for Song et al., a class of third-party processes and a measure of computational cost.

**Concepts covered.** Self-report accuracy and introspection; simulatability, through the comparison with third-party prediction.

**What it cannot express.** As stated, none of the criteria is a mathematical condition. Lindsey writes of metacognitive representations that "Demonstrating metacognitive representations is difficult to do directly, and we do not do so in this work." The criteria concern descriptions of "some aspect of its internal state", which Lindsey's accuracy criterion extends to "the internal mechanisms they use to perform calculations", but his experiments elicit reports of injected concepts and of whether an output was intended, not accounts of a reasoning process.

## What is missing

- **No definition of CoT faithfulness as actual causation or as an abstraction of the written steps was found.** Actual causes and explanations are defined over the variables of a given model, and causal abstraction relates a network to an algorithm; no paper treats the steps of a CoT as formulas whose truth and causal role are evaluated in such a model. CAuSE ties natural-language explanations to causal abstraction only for classifiers, and Yu et al.'s step-level probabilities of causation are defined relative to the correct answer.
- **No account of which causal variables a written step denotes.** Halpern-Pearl verdicts are model-relative, and Halpern & Pass allow causes that are formulas, but both presuppose the variables. Results from Dhanya Sridhar's group bear on how such variables could be fixed. Causal differentiating concepts are "interpretable latent directions in LM activations that must be changed to elicit a different model behavior", recovered as disentangled representations "under a particular assumption about the sparsity" ([Goyal et al. 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/5faf0f518f39e4d6b2b595e3ca2fe0c4-Abstract-Conference.html)). Sparse shift autoencoders "are identifiable from paired observations which differ in multiple unknown concepts, but not all" ([Joshi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12179)). [Saulus et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19594) show "that observations generated by a low-rank graph induce latents that form a causal abstraction" and give identifiability results for those latents. [Joshi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16698) add that counterfactual claims about what the model would have output "remain largely unverifiable without controlled supervision", which is the rung of a faithfulness claim about a single prompt, such as that the answer would have differed without a given step.
- **The vacuity of unrestricted abstraction.** Any definition of faithfulness through abstraction must fix the class of alignment maps: [Sutter et al. 2025](./#p=sutter2025non) conclude that if the linearity constraint on alignment maps is lifted, "we are left with no principled way to balance the inherent trade-off between these maps’ complexity and accuracy". A map from activations to written steps is likely to merge states with different downstream effects, which would violate the abstract invariance condition and call for the projected abstractions of [Xia & Bareinboim 2025](./#p=xia2025causal).
- **Information-based definitions that a post-hoc CoT also satisfies.** The sufficient-statistic definition of [Baker et al. 2025](./#p=baker2025monitoring), the sufficiency and completeness of [Jia et al. 2026](./#p=jia2026faithfulness), the conditional independence of [Wang et al. 2026a](./#p=wang2026case) and the usable-information definitions of monitorability and steganography are all properties of a joint distribution. A CoT that restates a precomputed answer can meet all of them, so they cannot separate faithful reasoning from rationalization without an interventional condition.
- **Concepts with only measurements, verbal criteria or components.** Post-hoc rationalization has no framework definition; the catalogue describes it in words, for instance as reasoning "produced after a certain conclusion has already been guaranteed" ([Lanham et al. 2023](./#p=lanham2023measuring)), and its concept-level formal rows reach it only indirectly: as the negation of a faithfulness definition ([Wang et al. 2026a](./#p=wang2026case)), as the failure of a necessity condition that "distinguishes genuine reasoning from post-hoc rationalization" ([Jia et al. 2026](./#p=jia2026faithfulness)), or as performative steps after the answer is already extractable ([Parekh & Goyal 2026](./#p=parekh2026drop)). Scheming and hidden objectives have components and settings but no definition of the concept; the nearest is a behavioral definition of defeat devices that covers evaluation-gated scheming ([Ferrara 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28863)). Introspection has verbal criteria. Obfuscation of a CoT is formal only by analogy, with opacity, unreadability and goal obfuscation in planning ([Kulkarni et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06137)). No definition of encoded reasoning requires that the model's computation causally use the hidden content; the closest, the non-transparent CoT of [Kaufmann et al. 2026](./#p=kaufmann2026aligned), is use-based but not causal: it compares what a human reads in the CoT with "what information the model’s downstream computation extracts from the CoT text". Honesty and lying have no definition in a causal framework: lying is defined formally only in logics outside the map, and honesty by a logical schema ([Evans et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06674)), by a fixed-point condition on performative predictions ([Oesterheld et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17601)) and, in the catalogue, behaviorally, as accuracy under pressure ([Wang et al. 2025 (lie detection)](./#p=wang2025evaluating)).

## Toward one definition

The evidence points to a composition of frameworks rather than to any single one. The five proposals below are conjectures, not results; each names the papers it rests on.

**Faithfulness as actual causation in a restricted abstraction.** One could read each step $s_i$ of a CoT $c$ as a formula $\delta(s_i)$ over the variables of a high-level model $H$ that approximately abstracts the network $L$ under an alignment map from a restricted class $\mathcal T$, and require the steps to be sound and complete with respect to the actual causes of the answer $A=a$. A possible form is

$$\mathrm{Faith}_\alpha(c)\iff\exists\tau\in\mathcal T:\ d_\tau(L,H)\le\alpha\ \land\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{Cause}_{(H,\vec u)}\big(\delta(s_i),A=a\big)\ \land\ \forall\psi\in\Psi:\ \big[\mathrm{Cause}_{(H,\vec u)}(\psi,A=a)\Rightarrow\textstyle\bigwedge_i\delta(s_i)\models\psi\big],$$

where $\Psi$ is a set of formulas of interest. This rests on the approximate abstraction of [Beckers et al. 2019](./#p=beckers2019approximate), the projected abstractions of [Xia & Bareinboim 2025](./#p=xia2025causal) for a lossy $\delta$, the restriction of $\mathcal T$ demanded by [Sutter et al. 2025](./#p=sutter2025non), the formula-level causes of [Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality) (Definition 4.1), the complete and sound credit sets of [Luo et al. 2026](./#p=luo2026removal), and the identifiability results of [Goyal et al. 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/5faf0f518f39e4d6b2b595e3ca2fe0c4-Abstract-Conference.html) and [Joshi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12179) for fixing the variables of $H$.

**Faithfulness and plausibility as explanation relative to two epistemic states.** A CoT could count as plausible to a reader when its content is an explanation of the answer, in the sense of EX1 to EX3, relative to the reader's model and epistemic state $K_R$, and as faithful when it is also an explanation in the abstraction of the model's own computation. Post-hoc rationalization would then be a CoT that is plausible but contains no actual cause in the model's abstraction, which is the reading the actual-causation search proposed: "an explanation that is sufficient in the reader's model but not an actual cause in the model's". Nontriviality (EX4) would require the CoT to tell the reader something the answer alone does not. This rests on Definitions 6.1 and 6.3 of [Halpern & Pass 2025](./#p=halpern2025causality) and on the general definition of explanation with model fragments ([Actual Causality, Section 7.3](page.html?doc=actual-causality#73-the-general-definition-of-explanation)).

**The monitor as an agent in a structural causal game.** The model and the monitor could be two agents, the model choosing the CoT and the action and the monitor choosing whether to flag. Monitorability would be the usable information that a monitor class has about a causally defined target, for instance whether a hidden-objective variable is an actual cause of the action under the game version of actual causality. Obfuscation would be the model intentionally causing the monitor's decision while the monitor ends ignorant of the target, deception of the monitor would be Definition 3.7 applied to it, and steganography would be the Receiver and Sentinel modeled as further agents. This is the completeness check's preferred unifier ("they are compatible with Halpern-Pearl and can include the monitor as an agent"), and it rests on [Hammond et al. 2023](./#p=hammond2023reasoning), [Ward et al. 2023](./#p=ward2023honesty) (belief, intention, deception, ignorance and actual causality in games), [Ward et al. 2024](./#p=ward2024reasons) (behavioral intention without utilities), [Xu et al. 2020](./#p=xu2020theory), [Anwar et al. 2026 (information theory)](./#p=anwar2026analyzing), [Anwar et al. 2026 (steganography)](./#p=anwar2026decision) and the polynomial-time monitor class of [Mohammadkhani et al. 2026](./#p=mohammadkhani2026hidden).

**Mediation for faithfulness, post-hoc rationalization and internality.** The conditional independence of [Wang et al. 2026a](./#p=wang2026case) has an interventional counterpart: a null natural direct effect of the instruction on the answer, with the CoT as mediator. Post-hoc rationalization could be a null natural indirect effect through the CoT together with a CoT that predicts the answer, and Lindsey's internality a path-specific effect that excludes the edges through sampled tokens. This rests on [Pearl 2001](./#p=pearl2001direct) (Definitions 4, 6 and 8), on Remark 53 of [Geiger et al. 2023](./#p=geiger2025causal), on [Lindsey 2025](./#p=lindsey2025emergent), and on [Beckers 2025](./#p=beckers2025large) for a semantics of counterfactuals over sampled tokens, which that paper gives only for interventions on the prompt.

**Introspection as resource-bounded privileged access.** Song et al.'s criterion could be stated as an inequality between the reliability of the self-report about an internal state and the best reliability achievable by a class of third-party predictors of equal or lower cost, a usable-information comparison in the sense of [Xu et al. 2020](./#p=xu2020theory), with Lindsey's grounding added as counterfactual dependence of the report on the state. This rests on [Song et al. 2025 (privileged self-access)](./#p=song2025privileged), [Lindsey 2025](./#p=lindsey2025emergent) and [Xu et al. 2020](./#p=xu2020theory).

## How this page was made

1. **Citation mining.** `runs/E_formal/cited_formal.py` scanned the reference lists of the 217 map papers for works cited by at least three papers and absent from the map: 225 arXiv identifiers and 234 title segments that match no map title exactly, 9 of which are in fact map papers cited with an appended arXiv number or a typo (`runs/E_formal/cited_outside.json`). Among them are works the searches later examined, such as Lindsey 2025 (cited by 5 papers), Anil et al. 2021 (3) and Evans et al. 2021 (3), and the title segment "chain of thought is not explainability" (11).
2. **Labels of the catalogue's formal rows.** Codex labeled each of the 350 formal rows of `definitions.csv` with a level (concept, measurement or both) and a formalism (`runs/E_formal/formal_labels.json`).
3. **Search and completeness check.** Five search agents, one per area (actual causation, causal abstraction, agency and honesty, information and verification, faithfulness theory in explainable AI), opened and verified 101 candidates. A completeness check built the concept-by-concept coverage table, searched frameworks the five areas had missed and added 13 candidates (`runs/E_formal/critic.json`). After one duplicate was merged, 113 candidates remain in `runs/E_formal/candidates.json`, 30 of them rated essential. The 23 framework papers are the essential candidates that were not yet in the map, except Halpern & Pearl 2005 (Parts I and II), Halpern 2016 and Chockler & Halpern 2004, whose definitions the [Actual Causality page](page.html?doc=actual-causality) already covers (chapters 2, 6 and 7 of Halpern 2016).
4. **Extraction and checks.** GPT-6.1 Sol, run through Codex, read the papers of three of the five batches, and Claude those of the other two after Codex reached its weekly cap; together they wrote the papers' map records and extracted 334 formal definitions with verbatim quotes, formulas, assumptions and locations. Claude checked each batch against the full texts and PDFs, applying 39 fixes, dropping 47 rows that were background, informal or out of scope, adding 4 and correcting 32 record passages; a final pass fixed 7 more rows (two formulas that KaTeX could not render, the two Cachin rows and three Yin rows) in `runs/E_formal/checks_render.json`. Of the resulting 291 definitions in `formal.csv`, all 291 quotes match the cached full texts verbatim; the 2 rows of Cachin 1998, first written from the abstract and from the restatement in Anwar et al. 2026 (steganography), were checked against the author's revised full text once it was cached.
5. **Counts.** Every count on this page was recomputed with Python from `formal.csv`, `definitions.csv`, `runs/E_formal/formal_labels.json`, the search files and the cached full texts.

6. **Claim checks.** Every factual claim on this page (134 claims) was checked twice against the papers and the data, each time by three Claude agents, the second time without seeing the first verdicts. The first check replaced 31 claims (22 overstated, 5 understated, 4 wrong) with the strongest statement the evidence supports; the second replaced 8 more (2 overstated, 4 understated, 2 wrong). Codex did not check this page, because it had reached its weekly cap, so the two checks are independent runs of the same model rather than of two models. The claims and both verdicts are in `runs/E_formal/claims_formal.json`.
